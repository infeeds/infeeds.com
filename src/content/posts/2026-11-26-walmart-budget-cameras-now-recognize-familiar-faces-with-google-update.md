Walmart's budget-friendly smart home cameras just got a significant upgrade. Google's latest Home update brings Familiar Faces support to two affordable Onn models: the $35 Outdoor Camera Plug-In and the $77 Floodlight Camera Hardwired. This means your home security setup can now recognize people around your house without breaking the bank.

Familiar Faces is Google's facial recognition feature that identifies people you've previously labeled in your camera footage. It's particularly useful if you have family members, roommates, or frequent visitors. The system learns over time and becomes more accurate as it gathers more data. Previously, this feature was limited to pricier devices like Google's own Nest cameras, so expanding it to Walmart's budget options democratizes home security in a meaningful way.

## What's Required to Use Familiar Faces

There's a catch though: you'll need a Google Home Premium subscription to actually use this feature. The subscription costs $12.99 per month or $129.99 annually. While the cameras themselves are affordable, the ongoing cost adds up if you have multiple devices. Still, for homeowners who want intelligent security without the premium camera price tag, this remains a reasonable solution compared to higher-end alternatives.

Beyond Familiar Faces, Google also rolled out a new remote control feature for these cameras and the Nest Cam Outdoor (wired, 2nd gen). You can now start or stop a 30-second security siren directly from the Google Home app while watching live video. This gives you an extra deterrent option when monitoring potential threats in real-time.

## Gemini Live Gets Smarter for Your Home

Separately, Google announced significant improvements to Gemini Live, its advanced AI conversational tool. The latest update brings faster response times through a new speech-to-speech architecture that creates a smoother, more natural interaction. But more importantly for smart home enthusiasts, Gemini Live now supports all your daily actions.

You can set timers, turn off the lights, adjust thermostats, and control other connected devices through conversation. This integration makes [smart home](https://infeeds.com/tags/smart-home) automation feel less like operating a dashboard and more like talking to an assistant. The rollout happens over the coming weeks, so not everyone will have access immediately.

These updates reflect a broader trend: Google is making its smart home ecosystem more accessible and capable. By bringing premium features like Familiar Faces to budget cameras, the company removes barriers to entry. And by integrating Gemini Live with smart home control, they're simplifying how people interact with their connected devices.

The smart home market remains fragmented, with different platforms offering different levels of sophistication. Google's approach here is competitive. They're using their [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) advantages in AI and software to create compelling reasons to stay within their ecosystem, even if you're using third-party hardware like Walmart's Onn cameras.

For budget-conscious consumers, this is genuinely good news. The barrier to entry for capable home security keeps dropping. Meanwhile, for existing Google Home users, these incremental improvements make their existing setup more valuable without additional hardware purchases.

The question now is whether Google's subscription model will become the standard for advanced smart home features, or if competition will push companies toward more inclusive pricing. Either way, the technology keeps getting better and more affordable, which ultimately benefits everyone who wants a smarter, safer home.
