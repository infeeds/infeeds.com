Genesis has done something unexpected with its new GV90 NeoLun electric SUV. While you might anticipate groundbreaking battery technology or revolutionary powertrain engineering from a high-end EV, the real innovation here sits on the sides of the vehicle: doors that actually think differently about how people get in and out of a car.

The GV90 NeoLun, which translates to New Moon, is Hyundai's luxury arm taking a swing at redefining what an electric SUV can be. Sure, it's got the performance specs and the tech features you'd expect from a six-figure vehicle, but it's the coach doors that steal the show.

## A Different Kind of Door Problem

You might wonder what's so special about doors that open differently. The obvious answer would be to just flip the hinges and call it a day, right? Wrong. That approach creates a safety nightmare in side collisions and makes the whole entry and exit process clunky, since one door would need to open or close before the other could move.

Genesis solved this with a "dual-motion hinge" system that's genuinely clever. The rear doors slide outward first, then rotate open. This engineering allows both front and rear doors to operate independently without jamming into each other. It's the kind of problem-solving that doesn't grab headlines but makes you appreciate the engineering when you're actually using it.

Safety was clearly a priority here too. The entire structure marries the doors to the vehicle body using dual high-strength steel beams. The cabin sits inside a reinforced frame that's 50 percent thicker than conventional vehicles and fortified with steel tubes acting as a concealed roll cage. For an SUV this size, those redundancies matter.

## Design That Turns Heads

The aesthetic pulls from Rolls Royce and Land Rover vibes without feeling derivative. Soft curves and a rounded rear give it an unmistakable luxury appeal, though the unnecessarily large front grille and those inverted-pyramid light strips are divisive. The coach doors themselves become part of the design language, creating that cavernous opening that screams premium positioning.

Underneath sits Genesis' new eMP platform, specifically engineered for cabin space and a refined ride quality. The 123.5 kWh battery pack is the largest ever fitted to a Hyundai Motor Group vehicle, delivering 311 miles of range on a single charge. That figure feels modest given the battery size, but fast charging technology helps here: a 350kW charger gets you from 10 to 80 percent in 22 minutes.

## Tech and Luxury in Every Detail

The interior reads like a luxury hotel suite on wheels. A 23.6-inch Cinematic Display expands to 24.6 inches when parked, creating what Genesis calls a drive-in theater experience. Meanwhile, a 25-inch heads-up display handles speed, navigation, and driver assistance functions. This is [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) that actually justifies its presence rather than just adding complexity.

The Bang and Olufsen Premier 3D sound system comes with a "virtual venue" feature that recreates acoustics from famous concert halls like Wembley Stadium. The cabin includes ambient lighting, massage seats, and something you rarely see: wood veneer flooring with radiant heating. The front seats swivel around for face-to-face passenger conversation, making it genuinely different from typical [luxury](https://infeeds.com/tags/luxury) vehicles.

A Smart Vision Roof adjusts its opacity across six separate zones. Interior materials span natural woods, cashmere, leather, and stone. The color combinations have those delightfully absurd luxury brand names like Veiled Indigo with Puglia Green or Tyrian Purple with Travertine Beige.

The dual-motor setup delivers 657 horsepower and 590 pound-feet of torque, probably adequate rather than thrilling for a vehicle this size. Active rear wheel steering up to five degrees helps manage the 208-inch length. Nobody knows the final weight yet, but this beast might rank among the heaviest SUVs ever built.

Genesis plans to launch the GV90 in early 2027, likely starting around $100,000. For buyers wanting an electric SUV that actually does something different, this could be it. The question isn't whether Genesis built a luxury EV, but whether they built one bold enough to matter in a crowded segment.
