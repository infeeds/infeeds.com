Boox just announced something genuinely delightful: the Picco, an e-reader so small it fits in the palm of your hand. We're talking about a device roughly the size of a standard playing card, with a 3.94-inch display and a weight of just 58 grams. For those keeping score at home, that's less than two ounces. If you've ever wanted a book-sized device that actually feels book-sized, this might be it.

The company is taking pre-orders now, with prices starting at $100 and shipments expected to begin in November. That's an aggressive timeline, but Boox seems confident about bringing this pint-sized reader to market quickly.

## What Makes the Picco Special

What really sets the Picco apart isn't just its diminutive size, though that's certainly the headline. The device features a monochrome ePaper display with a respectable 235 PPI resolution, which should deliver crisp text and clear images. Boox has equipped it with both physical page-turn buttons and touchscreen controls, giving you flexibility in how you interact with the device. You also get four-way screen orientation, meaning you can read in portrait, landscape, or however feels most comfortable.

The front light is adjustable with both warm and cool settings, so you can dial in the perfect lighting for your reading environment, whether that's late-night bedroom reading or bright afternoon sunlight.

Then there's the real stroke of genius: the magnetic ring case. This isn't just some protective shell. The design lets you attach the Picco to the back of your smartphone, turning your phone into a reading device without the added bulk of carrying a separate gadget in your bag. It's a clever solution to the portability problem that has plagued larger e-readers for years.

## Storage That Won't Hold You Back

Out of the box, the Picco comes with 16GB of storage, which is already plenty for thousands of books. But if you're the type of reader who wants your entire library with you at all times, the device supports microSD card expansion up to 2TB. That's an absolutely massive library capacity for such a tiny device, and it speaks to Boox's understanding that voracious readers sometimes want options.

## The Bigger Picture

The Picco is actually the first device in what Boox is calling its "Tiles" line. These are products designed to push beyond the typical tablet or e-reader form factor, bringing ePaper technology into more creative and unusual everyday use cases. The company's vision here is genuinely intriguing: what else could ePaper do beyond reading books?

This pivot toward unconventional form factors feels refreshing in a [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) market that often feels obsessed with bigger screens and more processing power. Boox is asking a different question: what if we made things smaller, more focused, and more specialized?

Given how charming the Picco appears to be, there's real potential here. If Boox can execute on this Tiles concept with the same thoughtfulness evident in the Picco's design, they could genuinely reshape how people think about e-readers and portable devices. The key will be resisting the urge to bloat future Tiles with unnecessary features and keeping that sharp focus on solving specific problems.

For anyone who reads regularly but has grown tired of lugging around a full-sized device or squinting at smartphone screens, the Picco deserves serious consideration. At $100, it's reasonably priced, and the November shipping date means you won't have to wait forever to get your hands on one. Whether this becomes a genuine category-creating product or a charming curiosity largely depends on execution, but Boox appears to be thinking bigger by going smaller.
