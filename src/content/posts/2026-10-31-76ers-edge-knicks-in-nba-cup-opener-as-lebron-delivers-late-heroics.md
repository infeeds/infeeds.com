The Philadelphia 76ers made a statement in the opening round of the NBA Cup, taking down the defending champion New York Knicks 98-93 on Friday night. It marked the second victory this season for the 76ers over New York, following their opening-night upset at Madison Square Garden during the Knicks' championship banner-raising ceremony.

LeBron James, the NBA's all-time leading scorer, delivered when it mattered most. The 41-year-old veteran scored 20 points and grabbed 9 rebounds while dishing 8 assists in what 76ers coach Nick Nurse called his best performance since arriving as a free agent in July. With the game hanging in the balance and the Knicks threatening to steal it away, James converted a crucial tip-in with just 11.9 seconds remaining to push Philadelphia ahead 96-91. It was vintage LeBron, combining size, basketball IQ, and clutch execution.

## A Dominant Performance from Maxey

Tyrese Maxey was the real offensive engine for Philadelphia, pouring in 27 points and setting the tone early. The 76ers controlled the tempo from the opening tip, and by the third quarter, they had built a commanding 14-point lead. The Knicks refused to go quietly though, mounting a furious fourth-quarter comeback fueled by Jalen Brunson's steady hand.

Brunson led New York with 24 points, including a step-back three-pointer with 1:32 left that briefly put the Knicks up 90-89. Karl-Anthony Towns added 22 points and 12 rebounds off the glass, but it simply wasn't enough against the balanced 76ers attack. New York fell to 2-4 on the season.

The victory came on an important night for the NBA, as Friday marked the beginning of the fourth edition of the NBA Cup, the in-season tournament format that has generated genuine excitement around regular-season [news](https://infeeds.com/tags/news) games. All group-stage games counting in the regular standings will run through November, with quarterfinals set for December 4-5 and semifinals on December 8-9. The championship game takes place December 11 at Hinkle Fieldhouse in Indianapolis.

## Elsewhere Around the League

The Dallas Mavericks proved their playoff credentials are intact, rallying from a double-digit deficit to defeat the Houston Rockets 116-111. Kyrie Irving led the charge with 35 points, while Cooper Flagg added 33 in a second-half performance that showed Dallas's championship DNA. Irving's three-pointer with 7:01 remaining proved to be the dagger, and the Mavs found answers whenever Houston threatened to pull level down the stretch.

Stephen Curry continued his assault on the record books, scoring 31 points to lead Golden State past Los Angeles 112-95 in San Francisco. The Warriors erased a stunning 23-point first-quarter deficit by outscoring the Lakers 38-15 in the second quarter to tie things up at halftime. Curry's performance pushed his career total to 26,674 points, moving him past Dominique Wilkins for 18th all-time. Luka Doncic had a mammoth night for the Lakers with 37 points, but it came in a losing effort.

Giannis Antetokounmpo put on a masterclass in Miami, delivering a triple-double of 31 points, 18 rebounds, and 10 assists as the Heat throttled the Indiana Pacers 145-125. In Boston, the Celtics showed their depth and firepower, romping past Chicago 125-111 with bench scorer Payton Pritchard erupting for 30 points alongside Jayson Tatum's 26.

Orlando's Magic showcased their three-point prowess, draining 18 treys in a 121-114 victory over Toronto despite Scottie Barnes' career-high 41-point explosion. Detroit added another chapter to the Nets' struggles, cruising to a 110-94 win in Brooklyn behind Cade Cunningham's 20 points.

Night after night, we're seeing which teams have staying power and which ones will fade when the pressure mounts.
