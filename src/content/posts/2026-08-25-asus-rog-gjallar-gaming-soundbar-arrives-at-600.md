ASUS has finally entered the gaming soundbar market with the ROG Gjallar, a premium audio device that takes a refreshingly different approach to competitive multiplayer gaming. Priced at $600 and now available in the US, this isn't just another soundbar slapped with gaming branding. It's genuinely designed around what gamers actually need: crystal-clear team communication without sacrificing immersive in-game audio.

## The Soundbar Built for Teamwork

The Gjallar's most compelling feature is its integrated microphone array and audio control hub. ASUS engineered this system to do something most soundbars can't: filter your teammates' voices from in-game audio while suppressing background noise. Imagine gaming with your squad without anyone hearing your keyboard clacking, your cat meowing, or your neighbor's lawnmower. That's the promise here. It's a genuinely smart solution to the age-old problem of balancing team communication with immersive audio experience.

The control hub itself is packed with functionality. An LCD display gives you quick access to volume, playback controls, input selection, and EQ profiles. No digging through menus or fumbling with your phone while in the middle of a ranked match.

## Premium Audio Performance

Named after the legendary Norse horn Gjallarhorn, this soundbar doesn't skimp on specs. The 5.1.2-channel configuration supports Dolby Atmos, delivering that coveted three-dimensional audio experience that makes explosions sound like they're happening around you rather than in front of you. The 6.5-inch subwoofer packs 65W of power and connects wirelessly via dedicated 5GHz to ensure zero lag. That last detail matters more than you'd think for competitive gaming where every millisecond counts.

The wireless subwoofer connection is particularly clever. By using a dedicated 5GHz band instead of standard WiFi, ASUS eliminates the latency issues that plague most wireless audio setups. Your low-end frequencies hit exactly when they should.

## Connectivity for Everyone

What impresses most is ASUS's commitment to compatibility. The Gjallar works with gaming PCs, Macs, mobile devices, PlayStation, Xbox, Nintendo Switch, and standard TVs. That's pretty much everything you'd want to connect it to. The ports reflect this versatility: USB-C, HDMI 2.1 with eARC, optical, 3.5mm AUX, and Bluetooth 5.3. You also get two USB Type-A ports for wireless dongles and other accessories.

This multi-device approach makes sense for gamers who juggle between platforms. You're not locked into an ecosystem.

## Current Availability and What's Next

The Gjallar launched in early July and is now available through Best Buy and ASUS's US website, though inventory appears limited. You can sign up for [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) notifications on ASUS's site to catch it when stock refreshes.

At $600, this is definitely a premium investment. But for serious gamers who spend hundreds on GPUs and peripherals, a soundbar that actually understands competitive gaming might be worth the splurge. The combination of studio-quality audio and functional teamwork features addresses a real gap in the gaming audio market. Most soundbars treat gamers as an afterthought. ASUS actually built this one around their needs.

For streaming enthusiasts and casual players, this might feel like overkill. But for esports competitors and hardcore multiplayer fans who want [gaming](https://infeeds.com/tags/gaming) audio that keeps pace with their ambitions, the Gjallar presents a genuinely thoughtful solution. The question isn't whether it's good, but whether you're willing to prioritize audio quality and team communication enough to justify the premium price tag.
