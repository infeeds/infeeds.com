## The New Cosmetic Procedure Taking Over Hollywood

If you've noticed something different about Jennifer Lawrence's face lately, you're not alone. The actor's recent appearance for The Cut photo shoot sparked widespread speculation about upper blepharoplasty surgery, with social media users noting that she looked almost unrecognizable.

But Lawrence isn't the only younger celebrity facing these questions. Bradley Cooper, Mandy Moore, Emma Stone and Matthew McConaughey have all been subject to similar speculation. While none have confirmed undergoing the procedure, the trend has sparked a broader conversation about cosmetic surgery and aging in [entertainment](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment).

"Upper blephs are the new veneers," one social media user lamented, capturing a sentiment that's spreading across platforms. The procedure, which involves removing or repositioning excess skin, muscle and fat from the eyelids, traditionally addresses concerns like tired-looking eyes, under-eye bags and obstructed peripheral vision. It's typically associated with older patients seeking functional and aesthetic improvements.

George Clooney famously joked about the procedure in a 2007 interview, saying "I think it's important to look awake." Jane Fonda and the late Dolly Parton have also publicly discussed their eyelid surgeries. But what's changed is the age demographic pursuing these operations.

## The Rising Trend Among Younger Adults

According to data from the International Society of Aesthetic Plastic Surgery, blepharoplasty ranked as the number one cosmetic surgical procedure globally. In the United States alone, the American Society of Plastic Surgeons recorded 118,978 eyelid surgeries in 2025, a nearly 19% increase from the previous year.

While adults in their 20s and 30s still account for under 3% of these operations, plastic surgeons say inquiries from younger patients are increasing. The procedure costs between $6,000 and $15,000, putting it out of reach for many but making it aspirational for those who can afford it.

Dr. Anthony Youn, a board-certified plastic surgeon in Michigan, suspects that several celebrities in their 30s and 40s have undergone the procedure recently. "The scars are easy to hide in the eyelid crease," Youn explained, noting that surgeons often combine upper blepharoplasty with brow lifts, cheek lifts and fat transfers for dramatic results.

## When Less Is More

Here's where the celebrity transformations raise red flags among medical professionals. Dr. Rod Rohrich from the Dallas Plastic Surgery Institute emphasizes that the goal should be natural-looking results, not the hollowed-out appearance some observers have noticed in certain celebrities' recent photos.

"Removing too much skin or fat can cause premature hollowing, dry eye symptoms or difficulty closing the eyes," Rohrich warned. Both surgeons agree that getting the procedure done too early can sometimes make a person appear older or markedly different rather than refreshed.

Rohrich's operating philosophy is that the best plastic surgery should be "invisibly visible." He doesn't recommend procedures where the benefit is minimal, and he stresses that changing beauty trends shouldn't be the primary reason for surgery.

## The Bigger Picture Beyond Surgery

It's worth noting that hooded eyes represent normal anatomical variation and are often an important part of a person's identity. The topic becomes even more nuanced when considering communities where blepharoplasty carries cultural significance, particularly in Asian American communities where double eyelid surgery remains popular and complex.

Factors like makeup, lighting, facial expressions, aging, weight changes, injectables and brow position can all make someone's eyes look dramatically different without any surgical intervention. Yet the celebrity speculation persists, fueled by social media commentary and the visibility of these transformations.

There's no right way to age, and no appropriate time to pursue cosmetic procedures. But as [cosmetic surgery](https://infeeds.com/tags/cosmetic-surgery) becomes increasingly normalized and discussed online, it's worth considering whether trends should drive our personal choices or whether our own comfort should.
