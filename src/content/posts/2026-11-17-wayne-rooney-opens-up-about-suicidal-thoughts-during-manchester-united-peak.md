Wayne Rooney has given a raw and candid account of his battle with depression during what should have been the greatest years of his life. Speaking on the Life at the Midpoint podcast with Gabby Logan, the Manchester United legend revealed he was "contemplating suicide" while at the height of his playing career, a confession that cuts through the glossy veneer of elite sports.

The former England captain's admission is particularly striking because it came during a period when everything appeared perfect on the surface. He had just married his childhood sweetheart Coleen, welcomed his eldest son Kai into the world, and was performing at the pinnacle of his powers for one of the world's biggest clubs. Yet behind closed doors, Rooney was spiralling.

## When Success Masks Suffering

"When I was drinking, I would lock myself in my house for two days and just drink," Rooney explained, describing the lowest points of his mental health crisis. "This is probably in my best part of my career playing wise." The contradiction is haunting: the man scoring crucial goals and lifting trophies was simultaneously wrestling with thoughts of ending his life.

What makes Rooney's story particularly important is his willingness to articulate how he masked these struggles. Athletes, especially men in high-pressure environments, are conditioned to project invincibility. Showing vulnerability feels like weakness, a crack in the armor that opponents might exploit. Rooney fell into this trap, hiding his pain even from those closest to him until he could no longer sustain the deception.

"You don't want to show any weakness, so you try to mask it," he reflected. "Even though I knew the position I was in, it was difficult to actually admit that I was in that position because I felt like you feel less of a man, if you like, for doing that."

The isolation that accompanied his depression was suffocating. Rooney didn't just withdraw from his family; he withdrew from the world. He didn't want to be seen in shops or on the streets. Even someone accustomed to performing in front of 75,000 screaming fans couldn't face basic human interaction. This paradox reveals how depression doesn't discriminate based on achievement or status.

## The Road to Recovery

Rooney's breakthrough came through therapy and the unwavering support of his wife. He credits Coleen with quite literally saving his life. "She could have walked away, it was that bad, but she stuck by me and helped me get through it," he said. By seeking professional help and opening up about his struggles, Rooney began the difficult process of reclaiming his mental health.

The 41-year-old's recent recognition as pundit of the year at the Broadcast Sport Awards suggests he's channelled his hard-won wisdom into his post-playing career. His willingness to speak openly about mental health battles represents a significant shift in how athletes approach these conversations, moving away from the old "tough it out" mentality.

Rooney's achievements remain staggering: 253 goals for Manchester United, 53 for England, five Premier League titles, and a Champions League triumph among numerous honors. But perhaps his most valuable contribution now is demonstrating that even those who appear to have it all can struggle profoundly, and that seeking help isn't weakness but wisdom.

His story serves as a reminder that the image we see of athletes on the pitch tells only half the story, and that mental health struggles deserve the same attention and compassion we afford physical injuries.
