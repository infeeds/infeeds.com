Jack Grealish's loan spell at Everton hit a bump in the road this week when manager David Moyes left the Manchester City winger out of the Carabao Cup clash with Newcastle United due to what the club described as a "breach of club discipline."

The England international, 31, missed an unauthorized training session earlier in the week without permission. "It's a club decision, it's something I don't want to speak too much about."

## Why This Matters for Grealish's Future

The incident raises questions about where things go from here for the player who seemed to be rediscovering his best form at the Toffees. Last season, Grealish made a genuine impression during his first loan spell, scoring twice and providing six assists in 20 appearances before a serious foot injury ended his campaign in January.

When he returned this summer for another loan deal, the early signs were positive. He featured in the Community Shield for City and appeared in their opening Premier League fixture. Upon rejoining Everton, he had already made six [sports](https://infeeds.com/tags/sports) appearances this season before the discipline issue arose.

The timing couldn't be worse. Everton's squad is already stretched thin following a disappointing transfer window, and Moyes has been counting on his senior players to shoulder more responsibility. Losing Grealish, even temporarily, is a luxury the club doesn't really have.

## The Bigger Picture

What's particularly striking is how much goodwill Grealish had built up at Goodison Park. Moyes spoke openly about how the player was "loved" at the club, and supporters had embraced him as one of their own. Everton chief executive Angus Kinnear even hinted at longer-term possibilities, suggesting there was potential for a more permanent arrangement if things worked out.

Now there's genuine uncertainty about whether this disciplinary action marks the beginning of the end for his Everton adventure. Grealish is in the final year of his reported £300,000-a-week Manchester City contract, so his future was always going to be complicated.

It's worth noting that Grealish did miss last Saturday's 2-0 defeat to Arsenal with illness, so he's had a rough patch physically and now mentally. Still, missing training without permission isn't something that sits well with Moyes, who clearly values professionalism and adherence to club values.

The good news for Everton is that the squad didn't allow the distraction to affect their performance. They dispatched Newcastle convincingly to reach the EFL Cup quarter-finals, with a trip to League Two Fleetwood Town awaiting them. Moyes praised his players for maintaining their focus despite the drama swirling around their teammate.

"The one thing about Everton is that it has got good values behind it," Moyes reflected after the match. "All the players stand up to the values on pitch and off the pitch."

When asked about Grealish's availability for Monday's Premier League fixture at Newcastle, Moyes gave nothing away. He's clearly not interested in fueling speculation or giving team news to potential rivals.

The real test comes when Grealish returns to action. Will he respond professionally and prove this was just a momentary lapse? Or has this incident genuinely damaged his standing at a club where he'd finally seemed to find his feet? For a player who cost Manchester City £100 million back in 2021 and has struggled to live up to that price tag, another stumble could prove costly.
