## Different Rules for the Powerful

If you or I displayed the behavior Jensen Huang stands accused of, we'd be finished. Berating employees, sleeping with subordinates, conducting extramarital affairs with someone identified by US intelligence as a likely Chinese spy who helped smuggle company products to China? That's a career-ending scandal for most people.

But Huang is not most people. He's a tech founder and Trump ally, and after less than two weeks out as Nvidia's CEO, he's back in the job. Trump, JD Vance, and other administration officials orchestrated his reinstatement, arguing the nation needs his leadership to stabilize markets and protect the AI sector.

Trump even posted an AI-generated image of Huang mimicking his own famous post-assassination-attempt fist pump, except Huang was holding a red bra. When the president of the United States is making light of a scandal involving alleged misconduct and possible national security breaches, something has gone badly wrong.

## The Market Over Security

According to reporting, Trump decided to intervene because he believed Huang's return could rally a struggling stock market. Nvidia, which manufactures the chips powering AI technology, has become something of a load-bearing wall for the entire economy. Its success or failure now affects whether investors panic or stay calm.

This is an extraordinary intervention. The executive branch explicitly pressured a private company's board to rehire a CEO whose alleged actions posed genuine risks to national security. That's not how capitalism is supposed to work. That's oligarchy.

What makes this even more striking is the hypocrisy baked into Silicon Valley's worldview. These tech executives promise to automate away human judgment, to make workers disposable and replaceable through artificial intelligence. Yet when it comes to themselves, suddenly one person becomes indispensable. The rules, it seems, only apply downward.

## Male Impunity and Sex-Pest Solidarity

Trump's own framing reveals something uncomfortable about what's happening here. When asked why he sympathized with Huang, Trump pointed to "the Payton Place part" of the scandal. Sex, he said, "sells better than stocks."

Consider who's saying this. Trump has been accused of sexual misconduct by more than two dozen women. He allegedly paid an adult film star to keep quiet about an affair. Now he's showing sympathy for a man accused of sleeping with subordinates and conducting affairs, one allegedly with a Chinese intelligence asset.

It's not just elite impunity at work. It's male impunity. The notion that powerful men deserve different treatment when sex is involved. That their accomplishments or business acumen somehow excuse their behavior toward women and their apparent willingness to compromise national security for personal gratification.

## What Comes Next

The fact that voters [rejected](https://infeeds.com/tags/news) datacenters in recent midterm elections should be understood as a proxy for something much larger: disgust with tech oligarchs imposing their will on the country with life-altering consequences. This Huang reinstatement will only deepen that anger.

It's also unclear why an administration claiming national security depends on AI companies would want one of the most critical firms led by someone potentially compromised by China. The logic doesn't hold. Unless, of course, the real logic isn't about national security at all. Maybe it's about protecting allies and rewarding loyalty.

When Democrats eventually gain congressional control, they should launch serious investigations into Nvidia, Huang, and the Trump administration's interference. The American people deserve to understand how their leaders prioritize campaign ties over genuine security concerns.

Until then, the message is clear: if you're rich enough, powerful enough, and connected enough, the rules simply don't apply to you.
