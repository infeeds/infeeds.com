If you're spending eight hours a day hunched over a desk, your chair matters more than you think. A good office chair isn't a luxury—it's an investment in your health. After seven years and testing over 60 chairs, the WIRED Reviews team has figured out what separates a genuinely supportive seat from overpriced marketing hype.

## What Makes a Great Office Chair

The best office chairs share a few key characteristics. Adjustability is non-negotiable. You need a chair that can move with you, not against you. That means adjustable armrests, seat depth, lumbar support, and recline angles. Materials matter too. A good mesh back dissipates heat so you don't end every video call looking like you just ran a marathon. High-density foam cushions provide comfort without sacrificing longevity.

Lumbar support deserves its own mention. Your lower back is where most desk-related pain originates, and a chair that can't be tailored to your spine's natural curve is essentially working against you.

## Branch Ergonomic Chair Pro: The Goldilocks Choice

WIRED's top pick for most people is the Branch Ergonomic Chair Pro at $499. It's not the cheapest, and it's far from the priciest, which is exactly why it wins. The adjustability is genuinely impressive. The armrests move up, down, forward, back, and angle inward or outward. Even better, the arm portions themselves adjust in and out, accommodating both narrow and wide frames. The seat depth pushes out for tall people and pulls in for shorter folks.

Tester Julian Chokkattu praised the high-density foam cushion and mesh backrest that actually follows your spine's curve. The lumbar support is aggressive, which some people love and others need time to adjust to. If you're over 6 feet tall, a $29 Tall Cylinder add-on keeps things proportionate.

It's sturdy, comes with a decent warranty, and won't require you to take out a second mortgage. The only minor complaint is that the recline tension knob doesn't do much, but that's a small tradeoff.

## Premium Comfort Has Its Place

If budget isn't a constraint, the Herman Miller Embody sits at the opposite end of the price spectrum at around $1,500. Yes, you read that right. But here's the thing: testers return to it again and again. The upright positioning supports your back in ways cheaper chairs simply can't replicate. It's adjustable in nearly every way imaginable, and it's gorgeous to look at with that distinctive rib pattern on the back.

The Embody comes completely assembled and backed by a 12-year warranty covering every single component. After five years of regular use, it still feels like new. That's the "buy once, cry once" mentality in action.

## Budget-Friendly Solutions That Actually Work

Not everyone can spend $500 or $1,500. The Staples Dexley proves you don't have to sacrifice comfort entirely at the budget end. At around $150, it's miles ahead of most cheap Amazon options. The all-mesh seat keeps you cool, it's reasonably adjustable, and it includes a headrest.

For [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) enthusiasts on a shoestring budget, there's also the Staples Essentials Task Chair at $120. It won't adjust to your body shape, but it won't collapse after three months either.

## Finding Your Perfect Fit

The middle ground between budget and premium is where the Branch Ergonomic Chair ($359) lives. It's the previous top pick and still fantastic. You lose the aggressive adjustability and forward tilt option, but you gain a chair that won't break the bank. Surprisingly, even a 6'4" tester found it comfortable.

Other premium options like the Steelcase Gesture ($1,510) offer exceptional comfort with an elegant design and easy adjustments. The Herman Miller Aeron remains iconic for good reason, though its controls feel like you're piloting a spaceship. The LiberNovo Omni Pro rounds out the newer additions to this year's list.

## The Investment Mindset

Here's what matters: your body spends roughly one-third of your life sitting. [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) workers often push that number higher. A chair that supports your spine, adjusts to your unique frame, and lasts for years isn't an indulgence. It's preventive medicine against chronic pain.

WIRED reviews multiple chairs with testers of different heights and body types because no single chair works for everyone. Your job is finding which adjustability matters most to you, what your budget allows, and then committing to a quality option rather than replacing cheap chairs every couple of years.

The WIRED Reviews team has put in the legwork. Now it's your move to stop sitting uncomfortably.
