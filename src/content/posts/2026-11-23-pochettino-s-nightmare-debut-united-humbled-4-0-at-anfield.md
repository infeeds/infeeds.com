## A Baptism by Fire at Anfield

Fourteen minutes in, arms folded, two goals down and reduced to 10 men, Mauricio Pochettino stood at the edge of his technical area listening to Liverpool supporters taunt him with chants of "Sacked in the morning." The reality of his new job was setting in fast.

Pochettino had arrived in Manchester just days earlier, touching down at 11:30 GMT on Wednesday after managing the United States team in Cincinnati. He flew directly from that final match against Haiti on a private jet, landing straight into what would become the joint-worst debut for any Manchester United manager in the club's history.

The 4-0 thrashing matched Lal Hilditch's 1926 record against Bolton, though Hilditch at least lasted 30 games before vanishing from management altogether.

## The Moment Everything Unraveled

It all fell apart in the eighth minute when Leny Yoro received a red card for fouling Liverpool's Alexander Isak. The decision set the tone for what followed: two quickfire goals, a defensive lineup in chaos, and a team that looked utterly unprepared for the intensity of Anfield.

Yoro's dismissal dominated post-match conversation, and Pochettino wasn't shy about voicing his frustration. The manager argued the red card was harsh, claiming even the world's best team would struggle playing 80 minutes with 10 men in such circumstances. Liverpool boss Andoni Iraola countered that the foul was clear and Yoro was the last defender.

The numbers tell a damning story. United sit 19th in the Premier League after 11 games, already 20 points behind Arsenal. Roy Keane's post-match analysis on Sky Sports was typically blunt: "Fans are applauding the players. Applauding what? Applauding rubbish." The former United captain questioned when players would take responsibility for their decision-making.

## Questions Over Squad Quality

There's a wider conversation brewing about the quality of players at Pochettino's disposal. Chief executive Omar Berrada claimed this season's squad is "fundamentally better" than the team that finished third last season. Few observers agree with that assessment.

Pochettino's biggest selection call was handing Matthijs de Ligt his first Premier League start in almost 12 months. It was a De Ligt error on a bouncing ball that forced Yoro into the fateful challenge. Meanwhile, United have kept just one clean sheet all season, in September's Champions League match against Azerbaijan's Sabah FK. That's a damning indictment of defensive solidity.

## A Manager's Defiant Response

Despite the scoreline, Pochettino emerged from the tunnel bullish about his team's character. "What I learned from my players today is that they like to fight," he said. "They are committed with the team, with the club, and of course they want to win games."

It's a valiant attempt to find positives in rubble. The Argentine manager had shed tears of emotion just days earlier when announcing his decision to leave the USMNT after six wins in limited matches. United have managed only 10 wins across all competitions this season.

## What Comes Next

Pochettino takes his team to Lisbon on Wednesday for a Champions League clash against Sporting. After that come Brentford, Newcastle, and Coventry in the [Premier League](https://infeeds.com/tags/premier-league). That Coventry match on 5 December represents a crucial juncture. If United remain in the relegation zone after that encounter, the pressure will become genuinely toxic.

This 4-0 defeat was perhaps the game United's fans and even Roy Keane expected little from. But there's a difference between low expectations and historical humiliation. Pochettino arrived with a decade-long flirtation with the club finally materializing into opportunity, only to face immediate [sports](https://infeeds.com/tags/sports) reality at its harshest.

The question now is whether he can rebuild quickly enough before the club's crisis becomes genuine catastrophe.
