Former NFL wide receiver Antonio Brown has agreed to a plea deal that will spare him from prison time in a high-profile attempted murder case in Miami. His defense attorney Mark Eiglarsh announced Wednesday that Brown will plead no contest to aggravated assault, a significant reduction from the original attempted murder charge that could have carried up to 30 years behind bars.

Brown is scheduled to appear in court on September 30 to finalize the agreement. Under the terms of the deal, he will receive probation with adjudication withheld, meaning he will not be considered a convicted felon.

## The Incident and Original Charges

The case stems from an altercation on May 16, 2025, following a celebrity boxing match in Miami. According to an arrest warrant, Brown allegedly grabbed a handgun from a security staffer and fired two shots at a man he had previously been in a fistfight with. The victim, Zul-Qarnain Kwame Nantambu, told investigators that one bullet grazed his neck.

Brown was extradited from Dubai in November 2025 after an arrest warrant was issued in June. The former athlete has maintained on social media that he was acting in self-defense and protecting himself and his jewelry from attackers.

## A Complex Defense and Strategic Decision

Eiglarsh's statement reveals the nuanced reasoning behind accepting the plea deal. "This was not an easy decision," the attorney wrote. "Antonio believes in his innocence. He believes strongly in his Stand Your Ground defense, and he believes he would ultimately prevail at trial."

Florida's Stand Your Ground law permits individuals who believe they face death or serious bodily injury to use deadly force without a legal duty to retreat. Brown's legal team apparently believed this defense had merit, yet they couldn't ignore the reality of the prosecution's offer.

"But when prosecutors put an offer on the table that transforms a potential decades-long prison sentence into a significantly reduced charge and probation, there was simply no way he could reasonably turn it down," Eiglarsh explained.

The victim's attorney, Richard Cooper, expressed satisfaction with the resolution. "We are satisfied that justice is served," Cooper said in an email. Notably, Nantambu has chosen to forgive Brown and will not cooperate with law enforcement. The victim made this decision after "considerable reflection, prayer, and consultation with his family and counsel," according to his legal team.

## Brown's Troubled History

This plea deal represents another chapter in Brown's tumultuous post-football life. The 38-year-old spent 12 years in the NFL as an All-Pro wide receiver, recording 928 receptions for over 12,000 yards and 88 total touchdowns. He won a Super Bowl championship with Tom Brady and the Tampa Bay Buccaneers in 2021.

However, Brown's career ended controversially during a 2021 game against the New York Jets when he removed his uniform mid-game and ran off the field, leading to his release. Beyond his athletic achievements, [entertainment](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment) coverage of Brown has increasingly focused on his legal troubles rather than his on-field accomplishments.

He has faced accusations including battery of a moving truck driver, multiple domestic violence charges, and failure to pay child support. This attempted murder case appeared to be his most serious legal challenge yet.

## Broader Context

Interestingly, the victim Nantambu gained his own notoriety earlier this year when he was convicted of a misdemeanor for disrupting Kendrick Lamar's Super Bowl halftime performance in 2025. He ran across the field at Caesars Superdome in New Orleans with a Sudanese flag reading "Sudan and Free Gaza" while serving as a dancer during the performance.

The Miami-Dade State Attorney's Office has declined to comment beyond saying details would be "fully clarified" at the September 30 hearing.
