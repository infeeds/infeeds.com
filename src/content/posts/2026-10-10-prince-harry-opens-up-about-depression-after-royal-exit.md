Prince Harry is shedding light on the darker side of his dramatic royal departure, revealing that he slipped into a serious depression shortly after arriving in Canada with Meghan Markle and their son Archie.

In a new MasterClass lesson about grief released this week, the Duke of Sussex opened up about an unexpected "adrenaline crash" that hit him just days after leaving behind the high-pressure environment of royal life.

## The Unexpected Breakdown

Harry explained that his body had become so accustomed to the constant stress of royal duties that when the pressure suddenly vanished, he experienced a severe physical and mental collapse.

"Away from the stressful environment where presumably my body was just pumped full of adrenaline, within two or three days, I experienced this adrenaline crash, something that I'd never experienced before," Harry shared during the online lesson called "Redefining Grief: Building Resilience Through Loss."

The prince described how his dependence on stress had become so profound that he couldn't function without it. "It was as if I needed stress in order to get the adrenaline to make my brain function because I became useless around the house," he confessed. "I actually slipped into depression for about two weeks and I felt like I couldn't function without adrenaline."

This vulnerability marks another moment in Harry's ongoing effort to normalize conversations around mental health and emotional struggle. The revelation shows that even stepping away from a demanding situation doesn't guarantee an easy transition.

## Grief and the Ghost of His Mother

The MasterClass lesson, taught alongside neuroscientist and professor Dr. Mary-Frances O'Connor, explores how grief and loss have shaped Harry's entire life. Central to his emotional journey is the death of his mother, Princess Diana, who was killed in a car crash on August 31, 1997, when Harry was just 12 years old.

Harry previously revealed in his memoir "Spare" that he harbored an unusual belief about his mother's death for years. He held onto a childhood fantasy that Diana had staged her own exit to escape the pressures of royal life and would eventually return in disguise to whisk him and his brother away.

"I believed that she had created this exit to get away from all the things that were destroying her life and that she was gonna reenter the fray in some disguise and then whisk me and my brother away," he explained during the taping.

Beyond this denial, Harry also struggled with basic happiness itself. He confessed that he felt guilty for experiencing joy, viewing it as a betrayal of his late mother's memory.

"I felt like I had to be sad and I had to be emotionally numb in order to prove to her that I missed her," Harry added. The admission reveals how grief can trap us in patterns of suffering, as though our pain validates our love.

## Looking Forward

With the 30th anniversary of Diana's death approaching, Harry appears to be working through his complicated relationship with her legacy. Sources close to the duke suggest he may be developing a documentary about his mother and has discussed several possible projects to mark the milestone.

A source told media outlets that Harry "has discussed a number of possible projects" and "is still considering the most meaningful ways to honour her legacy."

These conversations about mental health and grief are part of a larger [entertainment](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment) shift where public figures are increasingly willing to discuss their struggles. Whether through documentaries, memoirs, or masterclasses, these narratives challenge our assumptions about privilege and pain.

Harry's willingness to articulate his experiences with depression, grief, and emotional dysfunction stands in sharp contrast to the stoic silence traditionally expected of royalty. In doing so, he's inviting others to examine their own patterns of grief and stress, rather than suffering in silence behind closed doors and perfect smiles.
