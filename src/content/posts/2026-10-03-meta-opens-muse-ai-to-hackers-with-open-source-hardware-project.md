Meta's Muse personal AI agent has already captured attention for booking travel and handling everyday tasks, but the company just made a move that could energize the maker community. On Friday, Meta introduced Muse Gadgets, an open-source project designed to let developers build their own hardware that connects to Muse.

This isn't just a casual experiment. Meta is providing open-source firmware, a Linux software development kit (SDK), and project ideas to get tinkerers started. The possibilities range from giving Muse a color e-ink display to loading it onto an HDMI stick for your TV. Essentially, Meta handed developers the keys and said "build whatever you want."

## What Developers Can Actually Do

The beauty of Muse Gadgets lies in its flexibility. Users can grab a budget-friendly Raspberry Pi or an off-the-shelf ESP32 board and connect Muse to displays, buttons, sensors, actuators, and whatever else they have lying around. Meta even set up a Discord channel to support the community, showing they're serious about making this stick.

Meta proved the concept works by building Muse Home Link, a USB-C device that lets Muse connect to your home network and communicate with smart devices like speakers and TVs. The company manufactured 5,000 of these and is giving them away free to Muse subscribers while supplies last. Given that Nat Friedman's announcement post received nearly 30,000 views in hours, those freebies probably disappeared fast.

This move signals something important: Meta isn't treating Muse as just another [chatbot](https://infeeds.com/tags/chatbot). The company is building an ecosystem.

## The Bigger Picture: From Consumer to Enterprise

Muse Gadgets fits neatly into Meta's broader strategy to make Muse indispensable across multiple markets. The company isn't content serving casual consumers alone. Earlier this week, Meta announced Muse for Small Business, a free tier with usage limits that integrates with tools like Shopify, Dropbox, and Slack.

Meta also launched a new business unit called Meta Enterprise Platform specifically designed to push its AI offerings to corporations. This is a calculated play: dominate the consumer space while simultaneously building credibility with business buyers.

When you look at the full picture, Meta is essentially betting that AI agents need multiple access points to succeed. Some people want to chat with Muse on their phone. Others prefer interacting through smart displays. Businesses need integrations with their existing workflows. Developers and enthusiasts want to tinker and build custom solutions. Meta is trying to cover all these bases at once.

## Why Open Source Matters

By open-sourcing the hardware layer, Meta is making a smart calculation. They don't need to manufacture every possible device or predict every use case. Instead, they can let the community do that work. In exchange, every custom gadget that connects to Muse becomes a new touchpoint that locks users deeper into the ecosystem.

It's a page from successful platforms like Arduino and Raspberry Pi, which exploded precisely because makers could build whatever they imagined. Meta is attempting to recreate that lightning in a bottle, but with an AI backbone. The question is whether developers will actually embrace it or whether Muse Gadgets becomes another abandoned open-source project.

The fact that Meta is distributing free hardware and providing developer support suggests they're taking this seriously. But success depends on whether the maker community finds Muse compelling enough to build around, or whether they'll stick with more established platforms that already have thriving ecosystems.
