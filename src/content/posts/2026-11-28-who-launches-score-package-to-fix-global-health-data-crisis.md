The World Health Organization just announced something that might seem boring on the surface but could save millions of lives: the SCORE for Health Data Technical Package. It's not flashy, but it's exactly what the world needs right now.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, one harsh reality became impossible to ignore. Countries couldn't access the data they needed to respond effectively. Some lacked the infrastructure to collect health information routinely. Others had data sitting around unused because they didn't have the analytical capacity to make sense of it. This gap has been deadly, and WHO is determined to fix it.

"Countries must be able to measure progress to make progress," WHO Director-General Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said. It's a simple statement with profound implications.

## Why Health Data Matters More Than You Think

Accurate health data isn't just about statistics. It's about understanding what's actually happening in a country so resources can be directed where they're needed most. When you don't know where disease is spreading or which communities are most vulnerable, your response efforts become scattered and inefficient.

The pandemic exposed this vulnerability on a global scale. Response efforts were hampered by inconsistent data. Governments couldn't coordinate effectively because they were working with different information sets. This translated into delayed interventions, wasted resources, and preventable deaths.

The SCORE package represents WHO's answer to these systemic failures. It includes more than 90 universally-accepted tools and standards for health information systems strengthening. For the first time, all the key elements for optimized health information system performance are bundled into a single, harmonized package.

## A Practical Framework for Real Change

SCORE stands for Survey, Count, Optimize, Review, Enable. Each component addresses a critical phase in building better health data systems. The package is intentionally designed to be practical, actionable, and flexible. It's not some theoretical framework that looks good on paper but falls apart in implementation.

Governments can use SCORE to set benchmarks and improve standards in healthcare. The initial release includes two foundational documents, with additional components rolling out later in 2021, including the SCORE Assessment Instrument and status reports at global, regional, and country levels.

Dr Kelly Henning, Director of Public Health Programs at Bloomberg Philanthropies, emphasized this need: "The SCORE Technical Package provides country governments around the world with high-quality, essential technical guidance so that countries can strengthen their own data systems." Bloomberg Philanthropies is partnering with WHO on this initiative, recognizing that building better health information systems requires sustained investment.

## Why This Matters for [Public Health](https://infeeds.com/tags/public-health) Going Forward

This isn't just about responding to COVID-19, though that's certainly urgent. The world is facing interconnected challenges: the COVID-19 pandemic, the need to achieve the Sustainable Development Goals, and the Triple Billion targets WHO set for improving global health.

All of these require country-owned, disaggregated data. Governments need to understand not just national trends but what's happening in specific regions, among specific populations, broken down by demographics and geography. This level of granularity is impossible without robust data systems.

Dr Samira Asma, Assistant Director-General for Data, Analytics and Delivery for Impact at WHO, put it bluntly: "Our window of opportunity is closing, and we cannot wait to address data gaps." The time to act is now, not years from now when another crisis forces us to confront these weaknesses again.

## Building Back Better

COVID-19 has been a wake-up call. It's forced countries to recognize how critical robust health information systems truly are. But it's also an opportunity. As countries rebuild their public health infrastructure, they can do so with better tools, shared standards, and collaborative approaches.

SCORE embodies that collaborative spirit. It provides a common set of tools based on shared standards, allowing countries to learn from each other and avoid reinventing the wheel. When governments have access to comparable data across borders, they can identify best practices and adapt them to their own contexts.

The only way we successfully combat not just COVID-19 but future health threats is through solidarity and information-sharing. SCORE acknowledges that data systems are only as strong as the weakest link in the global chain.
