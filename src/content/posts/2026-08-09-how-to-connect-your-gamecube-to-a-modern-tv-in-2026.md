Nintendo's GameCube is hitting its 25th anniversary, and there's never been a better reason to dust off your old console and revisit classics like The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker and Metroid Prime. The problem? Most modern TVs don't have the analog inputs the GameCube was designed for. But don't worry, connecting your beloved console to a contemporary display is absolutely doable with the right equipment and a little know-how.

## Check Your GameCube Model First

Here's the critical part that many people miss: not all GameCubes are created equal. Between 2001 and 2007, Nintendo released two main hardware revisions. The DOL-001 model is what you want, while the DOL-101 variant is considerably more problematic.

Why? The later model ditched the Digital AV Out port that made modern HDMI adapters possible. Figuring out which you own is simple. Flip your console over and check the label on the bottom. If it says DOL-001 near the top, you're golden. Alternatively, look at the back of your console. The DOL-001 has two audio-video ports, with one clearly labeled "Digital AV Out." If you see this, congratulations. If not, you'll need a different approach.

## The Best Way to Connect a DOL-001 GameCube

If you've got the right model, investing in a quality HDMI adapter is your best bet. The Retro-Bit Prism HD Adapter stands out as the premium option. Sure, it costs more than bargain-bin alternatives, but you're getting superior build quality and a USB-C port for easy software updates. The Carby from Insurrection Industries and Bitfunx's adapter are solid budget-friendly alternatives that still deliver respectable image quality.

The actual connection process is refreshingly simple. Plug the adapter into your GameCube's Digital AV Out port, connect an HDMI cable from the adapter to your TV, and you're done. Most of these adapters come with remotes that let you access the on-screen menu to tweak settings.

Want even better image quality? Check if your games support 480p progressive scan output. Pop in a compatible title and hold B during startup. If progressive scan is available, enable it for noticeably sharper visuals.

## What If You Have a DOL-101?

Owning a DOL-101 isn't the end of the road, just a detour. Your best bet is a device like the RetroTINK-2X Mini. Take the composite cables that came with your GameCube, connect them to the RetroTINK, and run HDMI from there to your TV. It works, though it's not quite as elegant as the adapter solution.

Ultimately, your choice depends on whether you're feeling nostalgic about the original hardware or just want to revisit those amazing games.
