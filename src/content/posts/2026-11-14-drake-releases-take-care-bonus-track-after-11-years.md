Drake is giving fans exactly what they've been asking for. On the 11th anniversary of his critically acclaimed album Take Care, the Toronto rapper released the long-awaited bonus track "Hate Sleeping Alone" across all major streaming platforms, with particular emphasis on Spotify and YouTube where it was previously unavailable.

The move marks a significant moment for fans who have literally been waiting over a decade for official access to this particular cut. One devoted listener even admitted to sticking with Apple Music for the past five years just to hear this song, highlighting just how desperate the fanbase has been for its wider release.

## The Track That Fans Never Forgot

"Hate Sleeping Alone" was originally featured as a bonus track on Take Care's digital edition, sitting alongside the Lil Wayne-assisted "The Motto." Produced by Noah "40" Shebib and featuring background vocals from Trey Songz, the song perfectly captures Drake's signature approach from that era. It's a masterclass in genre-shifting, seamlessly blending rap and singing over moody, introspective production.

The lyrics are deeply personal and relatable. "I say I'd rather be with you, but you are not around," Drake sings on the chorus. "So I'ma call somebody up and see if they'd be down / 'Cause I hate sleepin' alone, I hate sleepin' alone." It's the kind of vulnerable songwriting that defined Take Care and cemented Drake's reputation as more than just a rapper.

The YouTube comments section tells the real story here. Fans are celebrating this release like they won the lottery. "Most underrated song in drakes discography I used to pray for times like this," one commenter wrote, capturing the sentiment perfectly. Another simply put, "Let's go, this finally released on Spotify" in all caps, which says everything about how long people have been waiting.

## Nostalgia for the Take Care Era

Take Care remains one of Drake's most influential projects, and the [entertainment](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment) landscape has been buzzing with Take Care nostalgia lately. The title track, which features Rihanna and achieved top 10 status on the Billboard Hot 100, recently resurfaced when Drake performed it during a Kick livestream. During that performance, he dropped a bombshell that'll disappoint fans: he told viewers they'll probably never see him perform the song again.

"This the only performance you're getting of this song probably, but s--t, welcome to Trainwreckstv, baby," Drake said to the chat. It's a sobering reminder that even iconic songs eventually fade from an artist's touring rotation.

But the release of "Hate Sleeping Alone" feels like a different kind of moment. It's not Drake moving on or closing the door on Take Care. Instead, it's an acknowledgment that this music still matters to people, that these songs have staying power beyond their initial release window.

## What This Means for Streaming Music

The journey of "Hate Sleeping Alone" to mainstream streaming platforms is emblematic of a larger shift in how artists handle their catalog. Bonus tracks and deluxe edition exclusives once created artificial scarcity. Now, fans expect everything to be accessible everywhere. Drake's decision to finally put this track on Spotify and YouTube is less about generosity and more about recognizing what fans have been demanding for years.

It also reinforces something important about [music](https://infeeds.com/tags/music) consumption in 2024: nothing stays exclusive forever. Dedicated fans will always find a way to access what they love, whether through alternative platforms or other means. Smart artists recognize this and release material when it serves both the fanbase and their own narrative.

So here's the question: if a song is good enough that people will actively search for it across platforms for over a decade, isn't it obvious that it deserves to be everywhere from the start?
