Jon Gosselin is speaking candidly about one of the most painful aspects of his life: his estrangement from six of his eight children. The former "Jon & Kate Plus 8" star opened up on the "Legally Goff" podcast this week, sharing details about his fractured relationships with his kids and the toll it's taken on him.

## A Father's Disconnect

Gosselin, who divorced Kate in 2009, has eight children with his ex-wife. His oldest are 26-year-old twins Mady and Cara, while his six younger children are 22-year-old sextuplets: Collin, Hanna, Aaden, Alexis, Joel, and Leah. The estrangement hits hardest when it comes to the twins.

"I haven't seen Mady and Cara in like forever," Gosselin admitted during the podcast appearance. "I have pictures of them, but I still wouldn't be able to pick them out on the street. That's how much I haven't seen them." He last saw them around age 13, and has missed everything since: graduations, proms, and the everyday moments that build a parent-child relationship.

The former reality star doesn't mince words about what he's lost. "I don't know anything about my twins, if they have boyfriends or girlfriends, or what favorite color they have now, if they drive a car. I know nothing," he said, his frustration evident.

## Blame and Alienation

Gosselin frames his situation as deliberate alienation. "One day I was an all-active everyday parent and it's upsetting I know and people are going to hate hearing this, but I was just cut out of their lives," he explained. He points fingers at multiple sources: his ex-wife Kate and the network that profited from their family's drama.

According to Gosselin, the narrative pushed by TLC painted him as someone who didn't care about his children. He believes Kate reinforced this story to their kids, particularly claiming she told Collin that Jon was "a shitbag" who didn't want to see them. "I believe that's exactly what she said to Mady and Cara and that's their mother and they believed her and that was it," he said.

There's a real tragedy in how [reality television](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment) can weaponize family dynamics for ratings. When a network profits from your pain, the incentive to resolve conflicts quietly dissolves.

## The Complicated Middle Ground

It's not entirely black and white. Gosselin does maintain relationships with some of his children. He's close with Collin and Hannah, and last saw four of the sextuplets (Joel, Aaden, Alexis, and Leah) in 2018. Collin, notably, has also become estranged from his mother Kate.

## Allegations and Responses

The family drama deepens with serious allegations. Collin has claimed he experienced physical and emotional abuse at Kate's hands, including being confined to a locked basement room with cameras. "My mother had a room built in our unfinished section of the storage basement," Collin told The Sun in 2024. "She had a room put up with cameras in it, a tiny window in the corner and it was bolt-locked from the outside. When my mother would put me in that room multiple times, she had zip-tied my hands and feet together and bolt-locked the door, turned the lights off and had cameras there just watching me."

Kate has denied these allegations, writing on TikTok: "I know who I am and my kids know who I truly am and to me, that's all that matters."

## What's Lost

Regardless of who bears responsibility, the real victims here are the eight children caught between their parents. Jon's admission that he wouldn't recognize his own twins on the street is heartbreaking. Whether through malice, miscommunication, or the corrosive effects of public scrutiny, an entire generation has grown up with fractured family bonds that [entertainment](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment) companies helped create and profit from.

The question isn't really who to blame anymore. It's whether any of them can rebuild what was lost.
