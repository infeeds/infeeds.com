Manchester United's chief executive Omar Berrada has waded into the debate surrounding the club's transfer strategy, defending the summer window signings while acknowledging that some of the criticism aimed at the club's hierarchy "has crossed a line".

In a rare interview with Sky Sports, Berrada spoke ahead of new head coach Mauricio Pochettino's first game in charge at Liverpool on Sunday. While he said he "understood" supporter frustration at United sitting 18th in the Premier League, he also insisted some of the criticism was fair but social media had amplified negativity around the club.

"Being in this position comes naturally with the criticism and scrutiny we're going to get," Berrada said. "We accept it. Some of it is fair, some of it does cross the line."

## Under pressure from all sides

Director of football Jason Wilcox has been particularly singled out by supporters, with social media calls for both Wilcox and Berrada to lose their jobs. Meanwhile, minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe faces regular chants calling for his departure, while opposition to the Glazer family ownership remains intense following their 2005 leveraged buyout that saddled the club with over £1bn in debt.

Wilcox has borne the brunt of blame for perceived poor summer recruitment. After a 2-0 defeat to Atletico Madrid in September, defender Lisandro Martinez said he "expected more from the technical director". Former boss Michael Carrick felt let down by a transfer strategy that targeted midfield while ignoring gaps at left-back and in attack.

Carrick wanted to pursue Elliot Anderson and Mateus Fernandes, but United pulled out when Nottingham Forest and West Ham's asking prices proved too steep. Anderson eventually joined Manchester City for £116m, while Fernandes moved to Tottenham for £85m. Instead, United paid a combined £118m for Carlos Baleba and Andrey Santos, who have started just seven Premier League games between them.

## Berrada's case for the defence

Despite the criticism, Berrada has stood firm on the club's decisions. He highlighted that the five summer additions - Baleba, Youri Tielemans, Andrey Santos, Karl Darlow, and the return of Marcus Rashford from his Barcelona loan - have "fundamentally" improved the squad.

"We went in knowing what we needed to do. Of course, there have been different dynamics at play and we've said consistently in the last few years we need to stay disciplined," Berrada explained. "We need to take the right decisions. We need to buy the right players, for the right value at the right time - in terms of what the squad needs."

He defended the reluctance to spend big simply for the sake of it. "There's no point in us spending £40m, £50m for players we're not convinced are going to add value to the team," he said.

Tielemans, one of United's few bright spots, scored his first goal in a 4-4 draw with Aston Villa and has recorded an assist. It remains to be seen whether the squad improvements prove enough under Pochettino's watch.

## Pochettino's early message

Pochettino, speaking to media for the first time as United boss, indicated he hopes to have influence beyond his official title of head coach, particularly in squad decisions going forward. He confirmed Bruno Fernandes will continue as captain for the Liverpool trip, despite the midfielder's poor form and uncertainty over a contract extension beyond summer.

The 54-year-old suggested rebuilding United's culture could take a decade, emphasizing the need to restore identity at a historic club.

With [football](https://infeeds.com/tags/sports) clubs facing intense scrutiny in the social media age, the question remains whether Berrada and Wilcox's measured approach will satisfy an impatient fanbase, or whether only results on the pitch will quiet the noise.
