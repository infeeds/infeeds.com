A 24-year-old gunman suspected of killing nine people across two locations in Poland has died in hospital early Monday morning, according to Polish police. The suspect opened fire in central Krakow's busy Main Market Square on Saturday afternoon before armed officers engaged him in a gunfight within three minutes, preventing further casualties.

## The Attack and Response

The shooting began shortly before 3 p.m. local time in one of Poland's second-largest city's most crowded areas. Six people were killed in the initial attack: four women and two men, including a British national and an Austrian woman. Four victims were Polish citizens.

Armed police responded with remarkable speed. Officers raced to the scene and engaged the suspect in gunfire, shooting him within just three minutes of the attack beginning. This rapid response likely prevented an even greater tragedy. The gunman was injured during the confrontation and subsequently hospitalized, where he died early Monday.

## Family Victims and Investigation

Polish authorities later discovered three bodies at the gunman's home: his 53-year-old father, 52-year-old mother, and 21-year-old sister. This brings the total death toll to nine victims across both locations.

Investigators discovered four firearms in the gunman's vehicle, including two AR-15 rifles and two handguns, along with several hundred rounds of ammunition. Polish media reported the suspect possessed a firearms permit and may have obtained the weapons from his family home. Authorities also seized at least four phones from his car and electronic equipment from his residence.

## Initial Motivation Unclear

While officials initially reported the gunman had been inspired by far-right ideologies, prosecutors later clarified that investigations into his actual motivation remain ongoing. A government minister described him as an ultra-nationalist without providing further details. The official stance has since shifted to caution, with Przemyslaw Nowak, a spokesperson for Poland's National Prosecutor's Office, stating that motive investigations continue.

## Heroic Medical Response

Two surgeons who happened to be eating near Main Market Square when gunfire erupted became unlikely heroes. Professor Michal Pediwiatr and his colleague Dr. Mateusz Wierdak rushed toward the chaos rather than away from it.

"We saw the shooting attack and decided to help the victims as soon as possible," Pediwiatr told the BBC. "We rushed into the area where the shooting was and started our rescue, because there were no ambulances available, there were no professionals around."

Dr. Wierdak used his hand to stem bleeding from one of two injured police officers, potentially saving the officer's life by keeping pressure on the wound until an ambulance arrived. Both officers have since emerged from comas.

## A Traumatized Community

Sixteen people were hospitalized following the attack, including five foreigners from Austria, Britain, Germany, Portugal, and Russia, plus two police officers and the attacker. Witness accounts paint a picture of sudden chaos in a normally bustling tourist destination.

Ruby, a visitor from London, described hearing gunfire and yelling at friends to run. Tom Brook, from West Yorkshire, was inside a nearby McDonald's when he heard multiple gunshots and saw people sprinting for cover. He later witnessed paramedics treating victims and saw a body covered in foil blankets.

## National Response

Poland declared two days of national mourning following the attack. Prime Minister Donald Tusk traveled to Krakow and described the incident gravely: "Until now in Poland we haven't had such drastic cases of terrorism. That's what we have to call this cruel attack on people's lives here in Krakow."

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham expressed solidarity, stating: "My thoughts are with the families of those who have lost their lives, everyone injured and all those affected by this senseless violence."

Main Market Square reopened to the public Sunday evening after security cordons were lifted.
