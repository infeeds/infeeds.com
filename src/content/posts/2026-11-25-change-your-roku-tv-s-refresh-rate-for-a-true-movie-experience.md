If you've ever felt that something was slightly off about how movies look on your TV compared to the theater, you're not imagining it. The culprit might be sitting right in your settings menu, waiting to be adjusted. Your Roku TV is likely running at 60fps by default, which is perfectly fine for everyday viewing, but it's not how movies were actually meant to be seen.

For nearly a century, cinema has operated at a standard of 24 frames per second. When you walk into a movie theater, that's what you're watching. That specific refresh rate has shaped the visual language of filmmaking itself, influencing everything from how cinematographers compose shots to how directors cut between scenes. There's a reason theaters maintain this standard across the globe. It's become the visual signature of cinema.

## The Difference Between 24fps and 60fps

Your TV's default 60fps setting isn't wrong, exactly. It works great for sports, news broadcasts, and most television content designed for the small screen. But when you're watching a film, you're experiencing it in a format it wasn't originally created for.

The jump from 24fps to 60fps might seem subtle at first glance, but it fundamentally changes the viewing experience. Movies displayed at 60fps can feel unnaturally smooth, almost soap opera-like in their clarity. This phenomenon, sometimes called the "soap opera effect," happens because your brain is accustomed to the slight motion blur and film grain characteristic of 24fps cinema. When that's removed, everything feels too clean, too immediate.

Changing your refresh rate is one of those hidden [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) tweaks that makes a surprising difference without requiring any expensive hardware upgrades.

## How to Make the Switch

The good news is that Roku has built this adjustment right into their system. It's not buried in some obscure developer menu. Getting there takes just a few clicks.

Start by opening your TV's main menu and navigating to Settings. From there, head into System, then look for Advanced System Settings. Inside that submenu, you'll find Advanced Display Settings. This is where the magic happens: look for an option called "Auto-adjust display refresh rate" and turn it on.

That's genuinely it. One toggle, and you're done.

What makes this feature particularly smart is that it doesn't lock you into 24fps permanently. The auto-adjust functionality means your TV will intelligently match the native refresh rate of whatever you're watching. Films will run at their intended 24fps, while most TV shows will display at either 30fps or 60fps, depending on how they were produced. Sports broadcasts stay smooth and responsive. News channels remain crisp and immediate.

## Why This Matters More Than You Think

Most discussions about optimizing TV displays focus on color accuracy, contrast ratios, and brightness levels. Those things absolutely matter, and they're worth tweaking if you're serious about your viewing experience. But they don't address the fundamental way motion appears on screen.

If you genuinely want to achieve what people call the "movie look," adjusting your refresh rate might matter more than any color adjustment you make. It's the difference between watching a film and experiencing it the way it was intended by the filmmakers who created it.

This single setting won't transform your entire viewing experience overnight, but it's exactly the kind of small optimization that separates people who just watch TV from people who actually pay attention to [how content is displayed](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech). It costs nothing, requires almost no effort, and delivers a genuinely noticeable improvement for anyone who watches movies regularly.

The question isn't really whether you should make this change. It's whether you can go back to 60fps once you experience how your films are supposed to look.
