Taylor Swift is getting back to where it all started. In a recent conversation with Recording Academy CEO Harvey Mason Jr. for the institution's Icon Sessions series, the pop superstar opened up about her deep connection to her country roots and why honoring that heritage has become increasingly important to her.

"I love my country roots more and more the further along this path I get and I want to honor them more and more," Swift said during the candid discussion. "I feel more tied to them, more loyal to them. The people that shaped who I am as an artist and as a writer, that all happened in Nashville."

## The Bluebird Cafe Dreams

Swift credited Faith Hill's discovery at the iconic Bluebird Cafe as a turning point in her own journey. "I had heard that Faith Hill got discovered at the Bluebird Cafe," she recalled of the venue where she herself was discovered at just 13 years old. "I started learning about the Bluebird Cafe, made it a personal life goal to get to perform there."

It's this kind of storytelling that has defined Swift's entire career. Throughout the Icon Sessions conversation, which spanned her prolific 20 years as a songwriter, Swift kept circling back to her self-titled debut album and its first single "Tim McGraw." She revealed something fascinating about that early track: "If I look back at the song 'Tim McGraw,' there are actually sort of hints at everything that was gonna happen in the next 20 years."

## A Historic Country Return

Swift's nostalgia isn't just wistful reminiscing. Earlier this year, she released "I Knew It, I Knew You," marking her first undeniably country song in years. The track made [entertainment](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment) history by becoming the first song by a woman to have every country radio station reporting to Mediabase agree to play it in the first week.

In a note shared through industry trade Country Aircheck, Swift expressed her gratitude: "Man, it's been a while! Thank you for making history with me. To infinity and beyond." The message felt like a homecoming, a declaration that she hadn't abandoned the genre that launched her to stardom.

The timing feels deliberate, or as one might say with Swift, perhaps not a coincidence at all. Her self-titled debut is celebrating its 20-year anniversary, and that milestone seems to have sparked something reflective in the artist. Memory became the central theme of her Icon Sessions performance, where she took the stage at the Grammy Museum in front of an audience of fellow songwriters and composers.

## Memory as Creative North Star

"It's songs that are connected to each other by the common theme of being about memory and reliving certain detailed memories of your life and the significance of those memories," Swift explained before performing a touching medley of "I Knew It, I Knew You," "August," and "All Too Well" on the same piano she used during the Eras Tour's surprise song sets.

That medley feels significant. Each song represents a different era of Swift's career, yet they're unified by the thread of remembrance and reflection. "If I was still on the Eras tour, I would do this medley," she added, suggesting this could become part of her live repertoire going forward.

What's fascinating here is how Swift is recontextualizing her entire body of work through the lens of memory and nostalgia. She's not trying to be someone new; she's acknowledging where she comes from and celebrating the evolutionary journey that brought her to where she is today. The country comeback isn't a step backward but rather an acknowledgment of the artistic foundation that everything else was built upon.

For longtime fans, this feels like a natural progression. For newer listeners discovering her roots, it's an invitation to understand why Taylor Swift remains one of music's most compelling storytellers.
