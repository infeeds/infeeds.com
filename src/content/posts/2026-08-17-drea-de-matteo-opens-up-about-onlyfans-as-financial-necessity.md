Drea de Matteo, best known for her Emmy-winning role as Adriana la Cerva on HBO's "The Sopranos," recently shed light on why she started her OnlyFans page, and it's a far cry from the glamorous image people might assume comes with being a beloved TV star.

In an appearance on Jamie Kennedy's "Hate to Break It To Ya" podcast, de Matteo explained that launching her OnlyFans was more than just a business move. It was, in her words, a "political statement." She described it as her way of pushing back against assumptions and saying, "Fuck you. I don't have to work." But here's the real kicker: she actually did have to work, and urgently.

## The Misconception About Wealth

Despite her celebrated role on one of television's most iconic shows, de Matteo doesn't fit the stereotype of a wealthy former TV star. "Everyone sees me coming, they're like, 'Here comes that rich bitch from The Sopranos,'" she shared. "And it's like, no, I'm not at all. In fact, you know, I live a pretty fucking normal life."

The real reason behind her OnlyFans venture? Survival. De Matteo had to open the page to get a lien off her house after it faced foreclosure. She wasn't seeking attention or extra income; she was trying to keep a roof over her head.

What happened next was surprising, even to her. After her kids encouraged her to post a bikini photo on her page, the response was immediate and substantial. Within 75 minutes, she had earned enough money to pay off the lien on her house. For someone facing the prospect of losing her home, that must have felt like a lifeline.

## A Pandemic Turning Point

De Matteo's financial struggles weren't new. In a 2024 interview with Variety, she discussed how OnlyFans became crucial during the pandemic while she was simultaneously dealing with judgment from her own family over her vaccination status. Her brother initially criticized her decision not to get vaccinated, but years later, when she revealed she'd had to open an OnlyFans account to save her home, his tune changed.

"Your body, your choice. I'm with you all the way," he told her. It's a striking reversal that highlights how perspectives can shift when people truly understand someone's circumstances.

## The Broader Context

De Matteo's story challenges several assumptions about [celebrity](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment) and financial security. Just because someone had a successful acting career doesn't guarantee lasting wealth or stability. The entertainment industry is notoriously unpredictable, and even award-winning actors face dry spells, unexpected expenses, and financial crises.

What's particularly interesting is how she framed her OnlyFans launch initially. She said it was meant to start a podcast because she feared being censored. That detail reveals something deeper about her mindset at the time and the various concerns occupying her thoughts during a turbulent period.

The whole situation also raises questions about the gaps in the entertainment industry's support systems. When a talented, respected actress faces foreclosure, why isn't there more institutional help available? Why does someone have to resort to a platform like OnlyFans to save their home?

De Matteo's willingness to be candid about her financial struggles and her decision to use OnlyFans is refreshing in an industry where many celebrities maintain carefully curated images. She didn't pretend to be thriving. She didn't accept charity quietly. Instead, she took control of her situation, even if it meant doing something unconventional and potentially facing judgment.

Her story serves as a reminder that financial hardship doesn't discriminate based on past success or talent. It also highlights how quickly circumstances can change and how adaptability, however uncomfortable, can be necessary for survival.
