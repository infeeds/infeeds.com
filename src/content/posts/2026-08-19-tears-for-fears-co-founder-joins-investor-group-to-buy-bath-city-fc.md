Curt Smith, co-founder of the legendary 1980s band Tears For Fears, is putting his money where his heart is. The musician has joined a 50-strong investor group attempting to purchase Bath City FC, the English soccer team that currently competes in the Southern League Premier Division South.

The proposed deal values the club at approximately 6 million pounds (8.1 million dollars), with both the club's board and supporters' society recommending the sale. For Bath City FC, this investment represents crucial capital that could transform the organization's future.

## From Rock Star to Soccer Investor

Smith grew up in Bath, a historic town in Somerset known for its Roman-built baths and proximity to British cultural landmarks like Stonehenge and Glastonbury Festival. Though he's now based in the United States, his connection to his hometown remains strong, particularly to Twerton, where he spent most of his youth.

"Here in Twerton, where I spent most of my youth, it is slightly under-served," Smith told the BBC. "For me it's exciting we can do something for the community around here."

The investor group is led by Andrew Stalbow, co-founder and CEO of a mobile entertainment company. If the deal receives approval and closes successfully, Stalbow would assume the role of club chairman. Smith's involvement appears primarily supportive rather than operational.

"If I can lend my name and make people a bit more comfortable in investing and coming to the club, then I'll do that," Smith explained. "At times you have to give back and this time I'm giving back to my home town."

## Musicians and Soccer: A Growing Connection

Smith joins an increasingly crowded field of musicians who've invested in [entertainment](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment) and sports simultaneously. Elton John famously served as chairman and director of Watford FC from 1976 to 2002, pumping millions into the club during his tenure. Ed Sheeran purchased a 1% stake in his hometown Ipswich Town and sponsors both the men's and women's team jerseys.

The hip-hop world has also embraced soccer ownership. Snoop Dogg is an investor and minority shareholder in Welsh team Swansea City, while Stormzy holds a stake in AFC Croydon Athletic. Norman Cook, better known as Fatboy Slim, supported his hometown Brighton and Hove Albion FC through difficult times in the late 1990s and early 2000s, with his Brighton-based record label Skint Records serving as the team's kit sponsor.

Perhaps the most notable recent example involves Hollywood actors Ryan Reynolds and Rob McElhenney with Wrexham A.F.C. Their involvement has yielded three consecutive promotions, all captured for the documentary series Welcome to Wrexham. Few celebrity investors have managed such visible success with such a large audience watching the process.

## The Tears For Fears Legacy

Smith's own legacy extends far beyond soccer. As co-founder of Tears For Fears with bandmate Roland Orzabal, he helped define the sound of the mid-to-late 1980s. The duo achieved two number-one hits on the Billboard Hot 100: "Everybody Wants To Rule The World" and "Shout," both featured on their double album Songs From The Big Chair, which dominated the Billboard 200 for five weeks.

The band's most recent studio album, The Tipping Point, arrived in 2022. It peaked at number 8 on the Billboard 200 while claiming the top spot on both Billboard's Top Album Sales and Top Alternative Albums charts, proving their artistic relevance continues more than four decades into their career.

Smith's involvement with Bath City FC represents something different from his musical pursuits, yet it reflects the same commitment to craft and community that defined his artistic journey. Whether his investment signals a new chapter for the club remains to be seen, but his willingness to back his hometown team demonstrates that not every artist needs to rule the world when they can help transform their community.
