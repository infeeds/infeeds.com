If you work from home, you already know the truth: a bad chair can ruin your entire day. Not just your productivity, but your back, your neck, your focus. After more than seven years of rigorous testing and evaluating over 60 office chairs, the WIRED Reviews team has narrowed down the absolute best options for every budget and body type.

The stakes are real here. Your mattress, your shoes, and your office chair are the holy trinity of comfort. Skip any of them, and you're signing up for unnecessary pain. The right ergonomic chair can mean the difference between a satisfying, focused workday and hours of fidgeting, neck cricks, and small dissatisfactions that compound into major problems.

## The Best Chair for Most People

The Branch Ergonomic Chair Pro at $499 strikes the perfect balance between value and performance. It's not cheap, but it's not outrageously expensive either, and it delivers on comfort, adjustability, and build quality in ways that matter.

What makes the Pro stand out is its obsessive attention to adjustability. The armrests move up and down, forward and back, and angle inward or outward. But here's the kicker: the arms themselves can also move in and out to accommodate wider or narrower bodies, then lock into place. The seat depth pushes out for tall people or pulls in for shorter folks. You can lock it upright or recline at several different angles. The lumbar support adjusts deeper or shallower. Even the seat tilt allows for active seating, a vanishingly rare feature.

Tester Julian Chokkattu praised the high-density foam cushion and the mesh backrest that actually follows your spine's natural curve. Yes, the stock seat might be a bit short for anyone over 6 feet tall, but a $29 add-on Tall Cylinder fixes that. Other customization options include fabric, vegan leather, or actual leather. The only real drawback? The recline tension knob doesn't do much in particular.

## When Budget Allows for Premium

If you can spend more, the Steelcase Gesture at $1,510 delivers comfort regardless of how you sit. It's beautiful, too, with several colorful upholstery options. The seat is genuinely cushy, and you can extend the seat depth forward with a simple twist of a knob. The recline knob offers satisfying clicks to indicate locked angles, and the armrest adjustments feel fluid and elegant.

The backrest aligns with your spine, and grabbing the tabs to adjust lumbar support is intuitive. The headrest upgrade is worth it here, unlike some chairs where it feels like an afterthought. The main drawback is heat retention since it's all foam, plus the upholstery may not hold up as well over time compared to other premium options.

Then there's the Herman Miller Embody. Yes, it's pricy. Yes, it might take a week or two to adjust to. But after five years of testing, it feels as good as new. The rigid seat becomes surprisingly pillowy over time, it whisks heat away effectively, and it's one of the most adjustable models available. The 12-year warranty covering every part makes the price slightly easier to swallow.

## Budget-Friendly Alternatives

Not everyone has $500 to drop on a chair. The Staples Dexley at $150 is miles better than the cheap Amazon alternatives that dominate search results. The all-mesh seat keeps you cool, it's reasonably adjustable, and includes a headrest. You can assemble it in about 15 minutes.

For ultra-tight budgets, the Staples Essentials Task Chair at $120 sometimes dips below $80 on sale. Don't expect premium adjustability, but the build quality is solid for the price, and the mesh back feels flexible despite being firm.

The [best standing desks](https://infeeds.com/tags/standing-desks) pair beautifully with any of these chairs if you're creating a complete [home office setup](https://infeeds.com/tags/home-office). The WIRED team has also tested mechanical keyboards, webcams, and laptop stands to complete the picture.

## What to Actually Look For

Adjustability is everything. Can you modify the armrests, seat depth, lumbar support, and recline? Does it accommodate your height and body type? Heat dissipation matters too, especially if you're in Zoom meetings all day. Materials like mesh and breathable fabrics beat foam in hot climates.

The reality is that everyone's body is different, which is why WIRED enlisted testers of various heights and body types for their evaluations. What works for a 5'1" person won't necessarily work for someone 6'4".

Investing in your chair is investing in your health, your productivity, and your daily comfort. The price tag stings, but consider how many hours you'll spend in it over the next five years.
