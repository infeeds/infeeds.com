## The Seasons of Your Career

We don't get to be young forever, but that's actually a good thing. The ancient wisdom found in the Book of Ecclesiastes reminds us that everything has a season, a time and purpose. Your career follows this same rhythm. Understanding which season you're in isn't just poetic reflection. It's the difference between chasing the wrong dreams and building something lasting.

I'm entering the final season of my career as CEO of a national home services brand, and I can tell you with certainty: the season you're in should reshape your priorities entirely.

## Spring: When Dreams Matter More Than Dollars

When you're young in your career, you're planting seeds. This is the springtime phase, full of potential and possibility. You're not yet drowning in quarterly reports or profit margins. The future is a blank slate, and you have time to dream.

I entered the roofing industry as a teenager with simple ambitions: become a successful contractor, work with family, build something recognizable in Ohio. That exact dream didn't materialize. In fact, I spent 15 years as a roofing contractor on the verge of financial failure for most of that time. But the dream evolved. What started as one vision transformed into something I never could have anticipated: Roof Maxx, now valued at eight figures with dealers across all 50 states.

Your early-career dreams rarely come true exactly as you envision them. That's not failure. That's the actual process.

## Summer: When You Cultivate and Invest

Big plans aren't enough. The summer of your career is when you actually do something about those spring dreams. This is the cultivation phase, where smart investment matters enormously.

Here's the thing most people get wrong: investment doesn't mean only money. Time is equally valuable, especially when you don't have much cash. My co-founder Todd and I didn't have liquid assets when we discovered the Roof Maxx formula. What we had were years of roofing experience that taught us to recognize market potential. We could see that homeowners needed a cost-effective restoration solution, not just expensive roof replacements.

We sold our existing company to fund this pivot. The move looked risky at the time. It felt like starting over. But it was actually the culmination of years of careful investment and observation.

We didn't cut corners on quality either. Our flagship product underwent extensive testing at Ohio State University. Our dealers received careful instruction to inspect each roof before recommending treatment. We included tune-up services that addressed minor damage like nail pops or isolated damaged shingles. This attention to detail created customer satisfaction that led to positive reviews and referrals naturally, without expensive marketing campaigns.

## Winter: When Legacy Becomes the Priority

I'm less than a month away from my 60th birthday. Winter has arrived. This shouldn't terrify you. Winter is when you take stock of what you've built and prepare to pass it forward.

Young entrepreneurs dream about the future. Mid-career leaders focus on time and money. But something shifts when you approach the later seasons of work. Your priorities genuinely change. You stop asking "What can I build?" and start asking "What am I leaving behind?"

Roof Maxx started as a family business. That family legacy matters more to me now than any valuation or market expansion. The proper arrangements for handing off leadership to the next generation have become my real focus.

This isn't unusual. This is natural. And it's valuable. [Leadership](https://infeeds.com/tags/leadership) in the winter years looks different because it should.

Your career's seasons aren't failures when they change. They're evidence that you're actually growing. The entrepreneur who still has the exact same priorities at 50 that they had at 25 probably hasn't learned much. [Business](https://infeeds.com/tags/business) success requires you to evolve what matters as your perspective deepens.

So when you find yourself in a different season than you expected, don't fight it. Lean into it. Your best work might not be behind you. It might just be waiting for you to stop being young.
