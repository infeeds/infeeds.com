Sylvester Stallone is getting real about one of his darkest struggles. The legendary action star recently opened up on The New York Times' "The Interview" podcast about his battle with bulimia during his peak Hollywood years, revealing just how far he was willing to push his body for the perfect on-screen physique.

## The Obsession Begins

It all started with a vision. Stallone wanted to look different from his peers, specifically different from Arnold Schwarzenegger's massive frame. "I started to think: Design your body like a jaguar, a cat," Stallone explained. "Not big like Arnold, but more feral and aggressive and very limber and athletic."

That vision, however, quickly spiraled into something much darker. What began as a fitness goal transformed into an all-consuming obsession that took a severe toll on his physical and mental health. When filming "Rocky III," Stallone dropped to an almost incomprehensible 2.6% body fat. At that extreme, his body simply couldn't function normally.

"I got down to 2.6% body fat when I was doing 'Rocky III,' and I couldn't hold anything down," he said candidly. "Or I didn't want to keep it down because I became so obsessed with staying at that point of fitness, literally an obsessive body-worship kind of thing."

This frank admission puts Stallone among other [entertainment](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment) figures who've battled eating disorders publicly. Princess Diana, Elton John, and Demi Lovato have all shared similar struggles, yet conversations about male eating disorders in Hollywood remain surprisingly rare.

## The Chemical Assistance

Stallone didn't shy away from another uncomfortable truth: achieving and maintaining those extreme physiques comes with chemical help. When asked directly about steroid use, his answer was blunt: "Oh, my god, you have to."

He explained his approach was methodical. "You do it in a sensitive way, six weeks on, six weeks off," he said. But he also acknowledged the reality that casual gym-goers often ignore. "People go, 'Oh, you don't get that way naturally.' No, you don't. But that also took a terrible toll."

Now at 80 years old, Stallone is dealing with the consequences. "I take a lot of pills to keep going," he admitted. The price of that obsession didn't end when the cameras stopped rolling. It continues today.

## Looking Back With Regret

If given the chance to do it all over again, Stallone wouldn't make the same choices. "It's come at a terrible price," he shared. "If I could do it over again, I would say, 'Let someone else carry the banner and don't do your own stunts.' I don't know what it was about hurting myself all the time."

This vulnerability from an action movie icon is striking. The man who defined toughness on screen is now questioning whether that relentless pursuit of physical perfection was worth it. His experience raises important questions about celebrity culture, [entertainment](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment) industry standards, and the mental health crisis hiding behind Hollywood's polished exterior.

Stallone's willingness to discuss bulimia, extreme dieting, and performance-enhancing drugs publicly deserves recognition. These conversations rarely happen in mainstream media, especially from actors of his stature. By speaking openly, he's potentially helping others recognize similar patterns in their own lives or the lives of people they care about.

The real question isn't whether Stallone achieved his physical goals. Clearly, he did. The question is whether those achievements were worth the lifelong health consequences and the years of obsessive suffering he endured.
