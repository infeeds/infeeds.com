SpaceX has apparently lifted restrictions on Ukraine's military use of Starshield, the military-grade variant of its Starlink internet service. According to reporting from the Wall Street Journal, Ukrainian Defense Minister Evgeniy Khmara confirmed to reporters that Starshield appears to be functioning normally again, though the military is still running tests and hasn't received an official explanation for why the restrictions were imposed in the first place.

## The 10-Day Connectivity Crisis

The timing of this restoration matters. Just 10 days prior, Ukrainian drones experienced a frustrating problem: they would lose internet connectivity whenever flying at speeds around 100 miles per hour. For a military relying heavily on unmanned aerial systems, this wasn't a minor inconvenience. It meant losing control of some drones entirely, while others had to fly slower to maintain connection, making them more vulnerable to enemy fire.

For context on modern warfare, [unmanned systems have become critical to military operations](https://infeeds.com/tags/defense), and internet reliability can determine tactical outcomes in real time.

## Trump's Phone Call and Political Pressure

President Donald Trump reportedly made a Wednesday call to SpaceX CEO Elon Musk requesting restoration of Starshield service. The timing and nature of that call raises questions about corporate influence in geopolitical matters. Both Trump and Musk have been vocally critical of Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky, with Musk recently posting on social media that "Zelensky has gone too far."

The Wall Street Journal had previously reported that Musk ordered SpaceX to throttle Ukraine's Starlink access. Some sources suggested Musk discussed this with the Trump administration beforehand, though that claim remains contested. The narrative around who initiated what and when becomes murkier the closer you look.

## The Offensive Operations Question

Musk's stated justification for the restrictions was that Ukraine had "violated Starlink terms of service and used it for offensive drone strikes to kill people." This claim created immediate controversy. Musk followed up by declaring, "Were Starlink to be turned off, the Ukrainian front line would collapse immediately. This is NOT a threat, it is simply a statement of fact."

The statement highlights an uncomfortable reality about modern military dependency on commercial [tech infrastructure](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech). When a single private company controls critical connectivity, geopolitical leverage shifts in unexpected ways. Whether Starshield was actually being misused according to terms of service, or whether those terms themselves are reasonable constraints during wartime, remains debated.

## Questions Without Answers

What's particularly striking is that Ukraine hasn't received an explanation for the original restrictions or their removal. The military is conducting tests to verify everything works as expected, but they're essentially operating in the dark about what happened and why. That's not confidence-inspiring for any military heavily reliant on a communications system.

The broader implication here is significant: private companies providing military-grade services create dependencies that can be weaponized or manipulated by political actors. Ukraine discovered this the hard way over the past 10 days. Whether the restrictions were justified, whether Trump's intervention was appropriate, and whether SpaceX should have this much power over military operations are questions that will likely echo through policy discussions for years.

For now, Starshield appears restored and operational. But the episode reveals vulnerabilities that no military wants exposed.
