## Two Days of Pomp, Minimal Substance

President Trump rolled out the red carpet for Chinese leader Xi Jinping this week, treating him to nearly 48 hours of ceremonies, state dinners, and carefully choreographed moments. The two leaders strolled through the National Archives examining the Declaration of Independence and Constitution. Trump cracked jokes about China's 6,000 years of history. Xi complimented America's "founding spirit." To anyone watching, it looked like two world powers hitting the reset button on their relationship.

But according to NPR correspondents who've covered China extensively, the pageantry masked a less impressive reality underneath. Yes, there were some wins. A temporary truce on rare earth elements got extended by two months into January, preventing China from completely cutting off U.S. access to materials critical for manufacturing and defense. Tariff reductions on roughly $30 billion of goods from both sides appear to be coming. But the real sticking points? They barely budged.

## AI and Taiwan: The Elephants in the Room

Artificial intelligence came up in talks, but the two sides remain fundamentally misaligned. Trump has repeatedly called for zero regulation of the AI sector, wanting to let market forces run free. Xi, by contrast, led with AI in his opening remarks, insisting it should "remain under human control." The Chinese readout urged cooperation while ensuring AI "serves human progress." Those aren't compatible positions.

The state dinner guest list told its own story. Seated at the table with Trump and Xi were CEOs from Meta, Google, Microsoft, OpenAI, Nvidia, AMD, and Apple. The message was clear: this relationship is about tech dominance and economic leverage. Taiwan remains another unresolved flashpoint, with the U.S. and China maintaining fundamentally different views on the island's future.

What's changed since Trump's first term is stark. In 2016, Trump accepted a congratulatory call from Taiwan's president, breaking decades of diplomatic protocol. [Senior China experts reacted with alarm](https://infeeds.com/tags/politics) at what seemed like a reckless departure from the One China policy. Fast forward a decade, and Trump is now lavishing praise on Xi while showing him his new Marine One helicopter.

## The Power Shift Nobody's Talking About

China is simply stronger now. The country holds more cards in trade negotiations than it did in 2016. Both American and Chinese state media coverage of the visit was remarkably similar this week, a rarity that itself signals something important. For Chinese citizens watching state media coverage, seeing the world's largest superpower treating their country as an equal sends a powerful message. This matters especially when China's domestic economy has slowed, and people are questioning whether their government can still deliver growth.

Trump seemed convinced that a personal touch would move the needle. He spent 90 minutes in formal meetings with Xi, then spent almost as much time showing him around the South Lawn and the new presidential helicopter. Xi, however, didn't reciprocate emotionally. He offered holiday wishes and formal remarks steeped in historical context rather than personal warmth. The contrast revealed different approaches: Trump favoring transactional intimacy, Xi preferring structural power dynamics.

The rare earth elements extension is telling. It expired in November and both sides wanted a longer deal, but mutual distrust scuttled that possibility. After all the pageantry and toasts, the bilateral relationship still runs on suspicion.

## What Comes Next

[Trade announcements may arrive soon](https://infeeds.com/tags/trade), but the real test comes when these leaders stop smiling for cameras. Iran, artificial intelligence, Taiwan, and economic competition aren't solved by state dinners or helicopter tours. The fundamental challenge remains: two superpowers trying to cooperate economically while competing strategically, with decoupling increasingly impossible but also increasingly necessary in certain sectors.

The visit showed that Trump and Xi can be cordial, that they can find common ground on narrow issues like rare earths. But it also showed that all the pomp in Washington can't bridge a chasm built on decades of competing interests and incompatible visions for global order.
