Prince Harry is breaking his silence about the hidden toll of his dramatic departure from royal life. In a newly released MasterClass lesson on grief, the Duke of Sussex revealed that he slipped into a two-week depression shortly after arriving in Canada following his January 2020 step-back announcement.

The confession comes as part of a broader conversation about loss, resilience, and the lasting impact of his mother's death. Harry shared that the emotional crash was unlike anything he'd experienced before, describing it as an "adrenaline crash" that left him struggling to perform even basic household tasks.

## The Adrenaline Crash Nobody Sees Coming

"Away from the stressful environment where presumably my body was just pumped full of adrenaline, within two or three days, I experienced this adrenaline crash," Harry explained during the online lesson titled "Redefining Grief: Building Resilience Through Loss."

What makes this revelation particularly striking is how it humanizes the prince's experience. For years, royal life had kept his nervous system in overdrive. The constant scrutiny, the protocols, the expectations. All of it created a baseline of stress that his body had adapted to. Then, suddenly, it was gone.

"It was as if I needed stress in order to get the adrenaline to make my brain function because I became useless around the house," he admitted. The bluntness of this statement cuts through the glamorous veneer often associated with the royals. Here was a man, newly liberated from centuries of tradition, discovering that his mental state had become dependent on the very system he'd just escaped.

## Grief and the Ghost of Diana

Harry's depression wasn't occurring in isolation. The MasterClass, taught alongside neuroscientist and professor Dr. Mary-Frances O'Connor, explores how grief shaped his entire worldview. Princess Diana's death when Harry was just 12 years old cast a long shadow over his psychological development.

For decades, Harry harbored an almost magical belief that his mother might still be alive. "I believed that she had created this exit to get away from all the things that were destroying her life and that she was gonna reenter the fray in some disguise and then whisk me and my brother away," he shared.

This fantasy wasn't merely nostalgic wishful thinking. It was a coping mechanism that prevented him from fully processing loss. More troublingly, it created a complicated relationship with happiness itself. Harry described feeling guilt whenever he experienced joy, as though allowing himself to be happy would betray his mother's memory.

"I felt like I had to be sad and I had to be emotionally numb in order to prove to her that I missed her," he explained. The psychological weight of carrying this burden for three decades cannot be overstated.

## Looking Forward to the 30th Anniversary

As the 30th anniversary of Diana's death approaches in August 2024, sources suggest Harry is contemplating how to honor his mother's legacy in meaningful ways. Reports indicate he may be developing a documentary about her life and impact.

What's fascinating about Harry's current phase is how he's attempting to reckon with his past while building a new life away from the palace. His openness about [mental health](https://infeeds.com/tags/mentalhealth) struggles stands in stark contrast to traditional royal behavior, which typically favors stoicism and silence. Whether this vulnerability represents genuine healing or ongoing struggle remains an open question.

The [entertainment](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment) industry and public observers will undoubtedly be watching to see what form these anniversary projects take and how Harry continues to process decades of accumulated grief.

One thing is certain: the man who once believed his mother would return to rescue him is now attempting to rescue himself through honesty and professional support.
