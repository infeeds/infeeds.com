The electric vehicle market has a pricing problem. While the average new car costs over $50,000, most Americans can't justify that expense. Slate Auto thinks it has the answer: strip away the luxury, slash the price, and let customers build their perfect [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) experience from scratch.

Meet the Blank Slate, a Michigan startup's answer to affordability. At just $24,950 for the base model, this minimalist EV truck comes in both truck and SUV configurations. No automatic windows. No built-in speakers. Just the essentials, customizable through over 200 optional accessories and a kaleidoscope of vinyl-wrap exterior options.

## Performance That Punches Above Its Price

Here's where things get interesting. You might assume a $25,000 EV truck would feel like a golf cart on the highway. You'd be wrong. The Blank Slate packs a 135 kW rear-mounted motor delivering 181 horsepower and 195 lb-ft of torque, with 2,000 pounds of towing capacity. That's roughly equivalent to a 2026 Ford Maverick. An 8-second 0-60 time won't turn heads at a stoplight, but for a vehicle at this price point, it's genuinely competitive.

Slate isn't trying to build a sports car. They're building a truck that works for real people with real budgets.

The real conversation, though, centers on one number: 205 miles. That's the Blank Slate's estimated range with its 63 kWh battery pack. Compared to competitors offering 280 to 450 miles, it feels limiting at first glance. But Slate is intentional about this limitation. A smaller battery means less weight, lower costs, and a vehicle engineered for practical daily driving rather than cross-country adventures.

## The Range Question That Might Not Matter

Consider this: the average driver travels 37 miles per day. Slate's 205-mile range covers that five times over. Even accounting for degradation and weather, most commuters could go an entire work week without charging. This is [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) philosophy meeting real-world practicality.

Charging infrastructure matters here. Compatible with DC Level 3 superchargers, the Blank Slate reaches 80 percent charge in 30 minutes. A standard Level 2 charger delivers a full charge in 5 hours. Even Level 1 charging through a regular wall outlet adds 20 to 50 miles overnight. The included cable handles both Level 1 and 2 ports, and Slate partnered with Treehouse to help install home charging systems for buyers who need them.

This isn't cutting edge. This is competent.

## Who Should Actually Buy This?

The Blank Slate won't appeal to everyone, and that's exactly the point. If you're dreaming of driving eight hours to the coast with one charging stop, this truck isn't your answer. But if you're commuting to work, running errands around town, and occasionally heading out for weekend adventures with a charging break factored in, the math suddenly looks very attractive.

A $24,950 price tag for a vehicle that can genuinely serve daily transportation needs changes the EV conversation. It's not about luxury. It's not about proving a point. It's about access.

Slate plans to begin shipping from its Warsaw, Indiana factory later this year. A $300 preorder secures your spot in the early delivery window, but be prepared: their design tool is deceptively addictive. Spending hours customizing your minimalist dream truck is part of the appeal.

The real question isn't whether the Blank Slate is good. It's whether an EV this affordable and practical signals a genuine shift in how manufacturers think about the market, or whether it's simply the exception that proves the rule.
