When HBO arranged a private jet to fly Emma Fogarty from Dublin to London for the premiere of The Slightest Touch, it wasn't a typical celebrity perk. Fogarty, 42, has epidermolysis bullosa (EB), a rare genetic skin condition so severe that 80% of her body is without skin and wrapped in protective bandages. Commercial flight would have been physically impossible.

The film documents Fogarty's completion of the Dublin marathon in 2024 with help from an unlikely companion: Hollywood actor Colin Farrell. For the final 4km, Farrell pushed her wheelchair, raising over 1 million euros for the EB charity Debra. It's a story of resilience, but more importantly, it's a story about two people from completely different worlds who found genuine friendship.

## An Unlikely Bond

Farrell and Fogarty met about 15 years ago at a charity dinner. Their connection was instant. "Humour. We were laughing from the minute we met, slagging each other," Fogarty recalls. What began as occasional texts evolved into something deeper, especially after Fogarty's left leg was amputated in 2019 following a cancer diagnosis. EB puts sufferers at extremely high risk of aggressive skin cancer.

"After I lost my leg, Colin was one of the only people who asked, 'How's your head? How's your mental health?'" Fogarty says. "I think people were too afraid to ask, or didn't want to. He was one of the few who did."

Farrell has been notably private about his personal life throughout his career. But for this [documentary](https://infeeds.com/tags/documentary), he made a conscious decision to open up. He welcomed cameras into his Los Angeles home, where his two sons appear in the background. He spoke candidly about his own struggles with addiction, starting at 13 or 14 in Dublin pubs, then progressing to "every drug you can think of."

He got clean in December 2005, waking up in rehab after filming Miami Vice. His son James, who has Angelman syndrome, became his reason to stay sober. "I realised he needed a dad," Farrell says in the film. The footage of James crashing the interview, diving over the sofa for a bear hug while munching crisps, is genuinely touching and unscripted.

## The Weight of Vulnerability

Farrell had genuine concerns about the project. "Is it going to seem like a puff piece? Or me trying to show that I'm a nice guy?" he worried. He didn't want to appear as some celebrity doing charity work for good PR. The reality is far more authentic. "We're friends in spite of Emma having EB, and in spite of any celebrity I have. We're friends."

Fogarty insisted on including intimate scenes of her bandage changes in the film. She explains, "When I walk into a room, people see my hands bandaged and my arms bandaged. It's intense, but what they see is bandages." Showing her wounds was crucial to helping people understand what EB actually is. Doctors compare EB wounds to third-degree burns. With characteristic humour, she joked: "We're not filming the bum! It's not that sort of movie."

Every minute of Fogarty's life is extremely painful. Daily nurse visits and bandage changes lasting two to four hours are routine. She takes significant amounts of painkillers just to manage. Yet her spirit remains unbroken. "Every minute of every day is very hard," she admits. "But you do have to keep going because you either live or you die. And I want to live."

Director Rachel Fleit approached the film conversationally, never planning questions in advance. She simply invited both subjects to tell their story from the beginning. "They're just pals," Fleit explains. "They have this banter that is just hilarious. At times, I was like, 'Am I making a buddy comedy?'"

Farrell has already committed to not watching the [film](https://infeeds.com/tags/news) again. "It's enough to live in my life," he says. Yet he'd do it all over again for Fogarty's story to reach audiences. Her spirit baffles him still after 15 years.

The Slightest Touch premieres exclusively on HBO Max on 26 November in the UK and Ireland.
