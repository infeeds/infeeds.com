Anthropic has officially cemented its place in startup history. The artificial intelligence company's underwriters exercised their full overallotment on Friday, bringing total proceeds from the public offering to $93.5 billion. That's a stunning $12.2 billion more than the initial Wednesday haul of $81.3 billion, and it decisively beat SpaceX's previous record IPO from June.

The company sold 1.127 billion shares of Class A common stock at $83 per share. Underwriters had the option to purchase an additional 147 million shares beyond the initial 980 million, and they grabbed every single one. This so-called "greenshoe" clause, common in IPOs, gives underwriters breathing room if demand runs hot. In Anthropic's case, demand absolutely ran hot.

## The Market Reality Check

But here's where the fairy tale gets complicated. Shares opened Thursday at $111.50 on the Nasdaq, only to slide throughout the day and close at $97.17. That's an 12.6% drop from the IPO price. On Friday, during a broader tech selloff, the stock sank another 12% in pre-market trading. Welcome to the real world, Anthropic.

Investors were clearly intoxicated by the AI hype machine when they bid up the stock at open, but reality has a way of catching up. The market's newfound sobriety reflects legitimate concerns: rising interest rates, skepticism about AI valuations, and political headwinds around the technology sector. These aren't minor speed bumps.

## Beyond the Public Markets

What makes this even more massive is the concurrent $26 billion private placement. A consortium of tech heavyweights including Advanced Micro Devices, Google, Microsoft, and Nvidia bought Class N common stock at the IPO price. This strategic move signals major players want deep pockets in Anthropic's future. Combined with the public offering, Anthropic has raised $119.5 billion gross before expenses. That's the kind of capital that can fund the multi-trillion-dollar AI infrastructure buildout everyone's talking about.

The timing was precarious. CEO Dario Amodei and his sister Daniela, who co-founded the company in 2021 after leaving OpenAI, had to navigate a treacherous landscape. Jensen Huang's shock ouster at Nvidia created uncertainty. Voters rejected AI-focused ballot measures. And the [business](https://infeeds.com/tags/business) community remained divided on whether the generative AI boom justifies the astronomical valuations.

## What's Next

Yet here we are. Anthropic is now a public company worth roughly $9.7 billion at the IPO price (though that valuation is already shifting given the stock decline). The company's Claude AI models have genuinely captivated corporate clients, proving there's real demand beyond hype.

When asked about the milestone, Daniela Amodei told CNBC something refreshingly grounded: "For us, it's like one stop on the journey. We think about this as, 'Wow, this is an incredible moment for Anthropic for our mission, but we're going to wake up tomorrow and we're going to be the same company.'"

That's the right mindset. An IPO is capital, not vindication. The real test comes in execution: Can they continue innovating? Can they compete with OpenAI? Can they justify their valuation to skeptical [business](https://infeeds.com/tags/business) leaders who are increasingly questioning AI spending?

Anthropıc just proved it can raise historic amounts of money. Now it has to prove it can spend it wisely.
