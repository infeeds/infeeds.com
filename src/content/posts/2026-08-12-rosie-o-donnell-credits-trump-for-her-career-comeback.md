Rosie O'Donnell is having a moment, and she's giving an unlikely source all the credit for it.

The comedian and longtime Trump critic recently sat down with Amy Poehler on the "Good Hang" podcast to discuss her renewed prominence in the public eye. O'Donnell, fresh off a well-received limited run off-Broadway and gearing up to guest-host "Jimmy Kimmel Live" starting August 17, attributed her newfound visibility to none other than President Donald Trump.

"I feel like recently I'm re-famous," O'Donnell told Poehler with a laugh. "I told my kids, 'Well kids, hold on 'cause mommy's re-famous.' I don't know how it happened. I think it's Mango Mussolini." She wasn't entirely joking.

## The Beef Gets Fresh

O'Donnell's feud with Trump is legendary, spanning decades and multiple public exchanges. Their latest round of hostilities started when the talk show host made some pointed observations about Trump's health during a recent interview. She questioned his fitness for office, suggesting he appeared unwell and shouldn't have access to nuclear codes.

"The fact that he's falling asleep, that he's very physically ill, that he doesn't look like he's long for this earth, that he shouldn't be the man in control of the nuclear code. Like, these are things that everyone around him knows," O'Donnell said in the interview.

Trump responded in classic fashion, posting an AI-generated image of himself as a doctor diagnosing O'Donnell with a "massive" case of "Trump Derangement Syndrome." It's the kind of tit-for-tat that has defined their relationship over the years.

## The Irony Isn't Lost

O'Donnell's tongue-in-cheek gratitude to Trump carries a pointed message. She suggested that if Trump understood how much he was actually helping her career by constantly feuding with her, he would probably stop talking about her altogether.

"If he knew how much he was helping my career and my life, he would shut up about me because, you know, he's a horrible creature," she said on Poehler's show.

It's a savvy observation about how [entertainment](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment) and politics have become increasingly intertwined in the modern era. Whether intentionally or not, Trump seems to be a publicity engine for those he publicly criticizes, and O'Donnell is just one example of a celebrity whose profile has benefited from his attention, however negative.

## Citizenship and Criticism

Last year, O'Donnell applied for Irish citizenship, a move that drew commentary from Trump's camp. A White House spokesperson couldn't resist a jab, saying "What great news for America!" when news of her application became public.

The exchange exemplifies the almost performative nature of their ongoing rivalry. Both seem to understand that their [conflict](https://infeeds.com/tags/celebrity) generates attention and keeps them relevant in a crowded media landscape.

## What's Next

As O'Donnell prepares for her guest-hosting gig on "Jimmy Kimmel Live," it remains to be seen whether she'll address her Trump renaissance on air. Given her comedic timing and willingness to joke about the situation, it wouldn't be surprising if the topic comes up.

The broader takeaway here is that in contemporary culture, enemies can be more valuable than allies. O'Donnell's recent resurgence in fame isn't coincidental, and she knows it. By publicly crediting Trump with her comeback, she's not really thanking him so much as highlighting the absurdity of their dynamic and how celebrity feuds operate in the age of social media and rapid news cycles.

Whether this latest chapter of their beef will lead to more barbs, more comeback opportunities for O'Donnell, or a temporary ceasefire remains to be seen. But one thing's clear: as long as they keep talking about each other, both will stay in the conversation.
