Will Jacks has quietly become one of England's most valuable white-ball assets, and his performance against Sri Lanka in the first ODI at Chester-le-Street proved exactly why. The 26-year-old delivered a masterclass in adaptability, scoring 46 not out with the bat and then dismantling Sri Lanka's batting lineup with career-best figures of 5-22 with the ball.

England posted 265 after being asked to bat first on a slow, low pitch that immediately told you this wouldn't be a run-fest. Harry Brook contributed 67 and Joe Root chipped in with 44, but it was Jacks who played the pivotal innings when it mattered most. After a middle-order collapse saw England slip to 211-7, Jacks refused to panic. Instead of swinging wildly at a difficult surface, he batted with maturity and responsibility, stretching the innings and giving England a fighting total.

## A Pitch Made for Bowling

What looked like a par score quickly became a fortress. Sri Lanka's batting order simply couldn't cope with England's disciplined bowling attack and the challenging conditions. The visitors were skittled for just 176, handing England an 89-run victory and maintaining their impressive recent form against this opposition.

Jacks took the big wickets you need to take in ODI cricket. Kusal Mendis and Charith Asalanka were both pinned attempting sweeps, and when the tail came out to bat, Jacks was ruthless. His figures of 5-22 are the best by an England spinner since Vic Marks managed 5-21 back in 1984. That's a remarkable stat that underscores just how well he bowled on a day when everything clicked.

## Building Momentum the Right Way

This wasn't vintage England, and that's actually the point. Captain Harry Brook and coach Brendon McCullum have been gradually shifting the team's mentality. For years, England could only win white-ball matches when they were piling up massive totals. These days, they're learning to win tight contests on difficult pitches against quality bowling.

Yes, England managed just 265 without hitting a single six batting first, a first since 2019. Yes, they were dismissed for the 12th time since McCullum took over the white-ball sides. But context matters. The opposition deserves credit for making life difficult, and England's willingness to adapt rather than force the issue shows genuine progress.

Sri Lanka were much improved in the field but far too timid with the bat. Their debutant leg-spinner Sachindu Colombage took 3-44 and showed genuine promise, particularly when he bowled Root and caught Buttler lbw. That's the kind of performance that gets noticed at international level, but it couldn't prevent the inevitable.

England now have 10 white-ball wins in a row against Sri Lanka this year. They can seal a third consecutive ODI series victory with a game to spare at Headingley on Thursday, with the final match taking place at The Oval on Sunday. After the chaos of recent summers, these back-to-back [sports](https://infeeds.com/tags/sports) series wins feel like genuine progress.

## Looking Ahead to the World Cup

Jacks' importance to England's white-ball setup continues to grow. As the team heads into two months of cricket overseas after this series, they'll do so on the back of another win. They've now beaten Sri Lanka earlier this year and toppled India in July. With Adil Rashid sidelined by injury and Rehan Ahmed still finding his feet at this level, the load on players like Jacks is significant.

The bowling was excellent across the board. Gus Atkinson returned 1-25 from seven overs, while Jamie Overton took 2-21 in just five, proving that the [cricket](https://infeeds.com/tags/cricket) talent in this squad runs deep. Everyone bowled hard lengths and were rewarded with consistent breakthroughs.

Enough questions remain about whether this squad can deliver on the biggest stages, particularly with the World Cup coming in a year's time. They'll need bigger totals and more consistent performances against stronger attacks. But days like this, when a player like Jacks can combine match-winning batting with world-class bowling, suggest England has finally found a formula that works beyond just entertainment value.
