Pascal Struijk will be available for Brighton's clash with Crystal Palace this weekend after successfully appealing his controversial red card from Saturday's match at Sunderland.

The Brighton defender was sent off after 57 minutes when referee Thomas Bramall was called to the monitor by VAR official Neil Davies. After reviewing multiple replays, Bramall showed Struijk a straight red for denying an obvious goalscoring opportunity as Sunderland forward Wilson Isidor attempted to run onto a through-ball.

Neither Bramall nor his assistant initially gave a foul. That's the first red flag. Yet VAR intervention led to one of the harshest decisions of the [sports](https://infeeds.com/tags/sports) season so far.

## The VAR Problem

The dismissal sparked disbelief among Brighton's players on the touchline, and rightly so. There was considerable doubt that Isidor would even reach the ball before Brighton goalkeeper Bart Verbruggen. This means it was a possible goalscoring opportunity at best, not an obvious one.

Red cards for denying obvious goalscoring opportunities through VAR review are mercifully rare because the decision involves too many subjective judgments. Last season saw only three such dismissals in the Premier League: Dominik Szoboszlai of Liverpool, Maxence Lacroix of Crystal Palace, and Marcos Senesi of Bournemouth (though that one was correctly rejected at the monitor).

With such a high bar set for VAR intervention in the Premier League, Bramall should never have been sent to the screen in the first place.

## A Judgment Call Gone Wrong

When referees are sent to review monitors, their judgment can become clouded, particularly when they've already decided not to award a foul on the pitch. This appears to be exactly what happened. Bramall's initial decision to let play continue should have held weight. Instead, he was persuaded by the VAR to overturn his own call dramatically.

It's a situation where the referee should have rejected the advice from the video booth. The bar for intervention should be unmistakable, not marginal. Brighton's appeal body clearly agreed, overturning the decision and allowing Struijk to avoid a one-match suspension.

Despite playing with 10 men from the 57th minute onward, Brighton managed to secure a 2-0 victory at the Stadium of Light. The result keeps the Seagulls firmly in contention, sitting third in the Premier League table, five points behind leaders Manchester City.

Struijk, who joined Brighton from Leeds United in a 20 million pound move over the summer, was making only his second Premier League start for his new club. The 23-year-old defender will now have the chance to make amends and help his side challenge [sports](https://infeeds.com/tags/sports) elite this weekend.

## Looking Forward

The successful appeal sends an important message about VAR decision-making in the Premier League. Not every close call should be escalated to the monitor, and referees must trust their initial instinct when the evidence remains unclear or genuinely ambiguous.

Brighton's strong start to the season, now with Struijk available, could prove significant in their title push. Manager Fabian Hurzeler will be relieved to have his defender back and fully fit for the demanding fixture against Crystal Palace.

This case will likely be referenced in future VAR protocol discussions. When technology exists to review decisions, there's an understandable temptation to use it. But sometimes the best decision is the one made in real time, without second-guessing.
