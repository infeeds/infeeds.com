Less than a week before Grand Theft Auto VI launches, a potentially serious packaging flaw has emerged. Online reports from ResetEra and X suggest that the download code printed inside GTA VI boxes may be visible from the outside if you shine a strong enough flashlight through the side at the right angle. It's the kind of oversight that could create real problems for retailers and consumers alike.

## The Visibility Problem

Rockstar Games chose to print the redemption code directly on the inside cover of the game box rather than including a separate paper insert or using a scratch-off coating. This design choice appears to have created an unintended vulnerability. Several photos have already circulated online showing codes illuminated through the box's exterior, though the extent of the problem remains unclear at this point.

Interestingly, the issue may not affect all copies equally. According to ResetEra discussions, US versions of the game include a backing that effectively camouflages the code from outside viewing. International copies, however, may lack this protective layer entirely.

## What This Means for Buyers

If this turns out to be widespread, the implications are troubling. All someone would need to access GTA VI is the exposed code, a console, and an internet connection. The physical box becomes nothing more than packaging. In theory, someone could photograph or memorize a code at retail without ever purchasing or opening the box, then redeem it on their own console. Unsuspecting buyers could end up with a sealed, unopened copy that's already been claimed.

This isn't an abstract concern either. Retail theft involving digital codes has been a documented problem across the [gaming](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) industry for years. Attackers have exploited everything from loose gift card packaging to poorly secured code displays.

## The "Physical" Game Question

## What's particularly revealing about this situation is how it highlights the awkward nature of modern "physical" game releases. Traditional physical copies included a disc or cartridge that served as a tangible key to unlock the game. Those physical media could be resold, gifted, or passed along. They had actual value as objects.

GTA VI's physical version is fundamentally different. It's really just an expensive way to purchase the digital version. The box contains nothing but a code. You don't own the game; you own a license that Rockstar can revoke or restrict at any time. If your internet goes down, your console breaks, or Take-Two decides to delist the game, that physical copy becomes worthless.

Engadget has reached out to Take-Two Interactive for comment about the issue and whether the company is aware of any problems with the GTA VI packaging. As of now, there's been no official response, though that could change once the company investigates reports.

The fact that you can't truly own or transfer a [digital](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) game license is already contentious among players and consumer advocates. But if codes can be stolen through packaging this easily, it adds another frustration: you might not even be able to play the copy you purchased.

Rockstar Games and Take-Two have significant questions to answer about the GTA VI physical release. Was this design choice cost-cutting? A manufacturing oversight? And how many copies shipped with this vulnerability? The company needs to address this quickly, especially since copies are likely already on shelves at retailers. If you're planning to buy a physical copy, you might want to wait for an official statement first.
