Vladimir Putin has reportedly proposed a summit with U.S. President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping to broker an end to the Ukraine war, according to the Wall Street Journal. The suggestion came during a phone call on October 9, with sources indicating the meeting could happen at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in November in Shenzhen, China. Notably, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy would not be invited to the negotiations.

The timing is significant. Trump's recent moves toward sanctions relief for Russia and restrictions on Ukraine's military capabilities have alarmed European allies and drawn criticism from within his own Republican Party. These actions paint a picture of shifting U.S. priorities that leave Kyiv exposed and vulnerable.

## Sanctions Relief and Strategic Confusion

Trump has defended his decision to temporarily lift sanctions on Russian diesel exports and signed an executive order waiving new Russia sanctions from legislation introduced by late Senator Lindsey Graham. The stated rationale: avoiding driving Russia away from the bargaining table. Yet Russia has shown little reciprocal commitment to peace talks.

Zelenskyy's frustration is palpable. He posted on social media that undoing these measures "seems wrong" to him, calling out the asymmetry in negotiations. Meanwhile, Trump fired back on Truth Social, accusing the Ukrainian president of not wanting to end the war.

The Institute for the Study of War wasn't buying Trump's explanation either, arguing that the executive order "will only enable Russia to continue financing and protracting the war in Ukraine without any proper incentives that may force Russia to engage in any meaningful peace negotiations." It's a damning assessment of [business](https://infeeds.com/tags/business) as usual masquerading as peacemaking.

## The Starlink Controversy

Perhaps more troubling is the restriction on Starshield, the military-grade version of SpaceX's Starlink satellite internet. Ukraine relies on these systems for military command and defense operations. The restrictions prevented platforms traveling faster than 100 miles per hour, effectively grounding critical drone operations.

Ukraine initially blamed Washington, though Trump denied involvement. SpaceX CEO Elon Musk shifted responsibility back to the U.S. government. Reuters reported that the White House issued the order, while the Telegraph suggested Musk and Trump collaborated on the decision.

Zelenskyy called it a "planned operation," noting that Ukraine tested Starlink connectivity in EU countries and found no such restrictions. The message was clear: someone in Washington deliberately handicapped Ukraine's defensive capabilities.

## Mounting Tensions and Unanswered Questions

The weekend brought renewed violence. Ukraine launched over 1,000 drones into Russia, targeting oil infrastructure in St. Petersburg. Moscow responded with ballistic missiles targeting Kyiv. This escalation came despite Trump's proposed "energy ceasefire" announced on October 11, which Russia ignored while Ukraine initially complied before resuming strikes.

Zelenskyy's concerns go deeper than tactical disagreements. Speaking to Bloomberg on Sunday, he expressed worry that "steps have been taken to convince Ukraine to capitulate." While carefully avoiding accusation, he suggested American partners might believe ending the war "at Ukraine's expense" is acceptable. That's not diplomatic language; that's a warning.

## What This Means

The reported three-way summit excluding Ukraine suggests a fundamental shift in how Washington views this conflict. If Trump, Putin, and Xi negotiate Ukraine's fate without Ukrainian representation, it signals that Kyiv's interests are secondary to great power bargaining. Combined with sanctions relief, Starlink restrictions, and pressure to halt energy infrastructure strikes, a pattern emerges that looks less like peace negotiation and more like managed surrender.

The question isn't whether Russia is ready for genuine peace talks. The question is whether Washington has already decided what peace should look like, and whether Ukraine will have any say in its own future.
