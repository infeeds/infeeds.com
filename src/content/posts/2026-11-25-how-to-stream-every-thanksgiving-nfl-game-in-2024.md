Death, taxes and vegging out on the couch to watch Thanksgiving football are three givens in America. This year's holiday slate is absolutely jam-packed with major storylines, division rivalries, star players and big-name halftime performers. The NFL even threw in an extra matchup on Thanksgiving Eve for good measure. With games spread across streaming services and traditional TV networks, figuring out how, where and when to catch all the action has become its own brand of sport.

The good news? We've got your complete roadmap to streaming every game without missing a single down.

## Thanksgiving Eve Kicks Off With Netflix

Today marks a historic moment: the NFL's first-ever Thanksgiving Eve game. Packers at Rams is streaming exclusively on Netflix at 8:15PM ET/5:15PM PT. If you don't have an active subscription yet, you can sign up for the ad-supported tier at just $8.99 per month. The best part? Signing up today also grants you access to this year's Christmas Day games, so you're getting double value.

The league is pulling out all the stops for the halftime show too. U2 will perform at SoFi Stadium fresh off releasing their new album, Carnaval De Luz. Unlike 2014's Songs of Innocence (which mysteriously appeared on everyone's iPhones), this one requires an actual choice to listen.

## Turkey Day Itself: Three Games, Three Vibes

On the actual holiday, the action kicks off at 1PM ET with Bears at Lions on CBS and Paramount+ ($8.99/month). This one features Wu-Tang Clan tearing up the stage for a halftime show executive produced by Michigan icon Eminem. It's a nostalgic throwback that appeals to football fans of all ages.

Next up at 4:30PM ET is the classic Eagles at Cowboys matchup on Fox. Here's the kicker: this game is available for free on Tubi, making it the only Thanksgiving game you can watch without paying a subscription. It's also on Fox One ($19.99/month) if you prefer that route. Texas native Miranda Lambert performs at the Salvation Army Red Kettle Kickoff Halftime Show, honoring 30 years of that annual tradition.

The nightcap features two of the league's premier quarterbacks in Chiefs at Bills at 8:20PM ET on NBC and Peacock (Premium tier, $12.99/month). The Goo Goo Dolls headline halftime in Buffalo, keeping it local and low-key.

## Cutting the Cord Without Losing Football

If individual subscriptions feel overwhelming, [streaming services](https://infeeds.com/tags/streaming) like YouTube TV, Hulu + Live TV and DIRECTV all carry the full Thanksgiving slate. YouTube TV's Sports tier starts at $49.99/month, while Hulu + Live TV begins at $99.99/month. These options let you watch across multiple networks without juggling separate apps.

Sling TV Select ($19.99/month) or higher tiers also carry the Eagles and Chiefs games if you want a cheaper alternative to the big bundles.

## Black Friday Wraps It Up

On Black Friday, things cool down slightly. Broncos at Steelers streams exclusively on Prime Video at 3PM ET. Amazon doesn't require a full Prime membership for this one, offering a standalone subscription for $8.99/month. Fair warning: this matchup skips the halftime show, but two of the league's top teams should provide sufficient entertainment.

The streaming landscape for [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech)-savvy sports fans has become genuinely complex, but this year's Thanksgiving coverage actually showcases the benefits of that fragmentation. Netflix gets an exclusive premiere event, Paramount+ brings Wu-Tang Clan, and free options exist on Tubi. Meanwhile, traditional cable alternatives remain solid for viewers who don't want to juggle multiple subscriptions.

The real question isn't whether you can watch every game. It's whether you should actually try to watch every game.
