Black Friday has officially lost its one-day identity. What used to be a single Friday in November has morphed into a sprawling season of deals that now kicks off before Halloween. The question isn't whether you'll find discounts, but whether the price you see today will be undercut next month.

WIRED's reviews team has been tracking prices on their favorite gadgets year-round, and they're sharing the early Black Friday deals that have actually caught their attention so far. The catch? They're being refreshingly honest about which items might dip even lower come official Black Friday.

## The Apple Ecosystem Bets

If you're deep in the Apple world, there are some solid early picks. AirTags are sitting at their lowest single-tag price, though this happens frequently enough that it's not shocking. The real question is whether you actually need them or just like the idea of them.

AirPods Pro 3 are tempting if you want seamless iPhone integration, impressive noise cancellation, and real-time translation features. But the reviews team admits they hesitate to call the current price a "great deal" because you'll probably see another $20 off by Black Friday proper.

The AirPods Max 2 present an interesting case. At $100 off, they suddenly compete better with Sony and Bose options that reviewers typically prefer. Still, expect at least another $30 discount when the real Black Friday madness hits.

## Beyond Apple: Headphones and Chargers

For non-Apple loyalists, the Sony WH-1000XM6 headphones are worth serious consideration. With 30-hour battery life, excellent noise cancellation, and customizable touch controls, they've hit their lowest price ever. This isn't a "usually goes lower" situation. This is genuinely rare.

The accessory game has gotten weirdly fun. A [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) charger with adjustable height that doubles as a video tripod is down to $60, its lowest price at Best Buy. Moft's origami-style phone stands and wallets are up to 30% off during their early sale. And there's a wireless charger with an actual robot face that changes expressions based on charging status. Because apparently gadgets needed personalities.

The EcoFlow Rapid Pro power bank deserves attention if you work on the go. With 27,650 mAh capacity that airlines actually allow, three USB-C ports, and a retractable cable, it's one of those products that genuinely improves your life once you have it.

## Home Tech That Actually Matters

## The Vacuum and Robot Situation

If your floors need saving, there are some legitimate bargains here. A cordless Dyson with three cleaning modes, HEPA filtration, and dual rotating brush bars is cheaper than ever. The Eufy Omni C28 robot vacuum that mops, maps multiple floors, and self-cleans is currently below its usual $500 price point, despite an $800 MSRP.

Outside the home, a robot mower for challenging terrain is at its lowest price ever. It handles steep slopes and tree cover using GPS, lidar, and AI vision. That's the kind of innovation that makes you wonder how you survived without it.

The Rocco minifridge might seem frivolous until you imagine ice-cold Diet Cokes at midnight or a wine fridge in your bedroom. Every color is discounted. Sometimes the best deals are the ones that make life slightly more pleasant.

## The Smart Home Ecosystem

A Tapo security camera recording at 2K with motion detection just dropped to $60, its lowest price in a year. Blink's wired video doorbell is the cheapest option worth considering at sale price, though you'll need a subscription for person detection and cloud storage.

Eufy's permanent outdoor lights support Matter, which means you can tie them into any smart home ecosystem. The 200-foot string is at its lowest price ever. Imagine never climbing a ladder to string holiday lights again.

The Everblog HomeCal digital wall calendar is perfect for people who hate subscriptions. It syncs calendars, displays family chores, and works as a photo frame. Clip the coupon for an extra $50 off.

The Kiwibit Bird Feeder 2 captures 4K video with AI bird identification and charges itself via solar panel. Yes, you can watch birds in stunning quality while your feeder charges itself.

## The Real Question

Here's what the [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) world won't tell you: some deals peak in November, while others dip lower in December. WIRED's honest approach is refreshing because they admit which items will probably drop further. The trick is distinguishing between genuine early wins and things you should wait on.

But there's another consideration nobody mentions. Sometimes the best deal is buying something now that will make your life better for the next year.
