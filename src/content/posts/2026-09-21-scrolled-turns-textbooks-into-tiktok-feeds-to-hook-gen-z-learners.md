Gen Z gets a lot of grief for their attention spans. Doomscrolling, TikTok addiction, goldfish brain the whole narrative goes. But what if the real problem isn't that young people can't focus, but that we're asking them to focus on the wrong medium?

That's the thesis behind ScrollEd, a Palo Alto startup that's crashing TechCrunch Disrupt 2026 with a deceptively simple idea: if students won't open textbooks, make the textbook come to them as a social feed.

## Turning Boredom Into Engagement

The team built an app that converts any text file, PDF, or boring textbook into a vertical feed that looks and feels like Instagram Reels or TikTok. Swipe down and you get AI-generated video, audio, text, or interactive quizzes. Swipe sideways to go deeper on a topic. Hit the end and there's a quiz waiting.

It sounds gimmicky on the surface. But co-founder Utsav Gupta frames it differently: "The younger generation is increasingly going short form, vertical feed only, and this poses challenges for any kind of media that requires depth. We want to meet them where they are."

Gupta and his co-founder wife Rebecca Neff both study at top universities (Stanford and UPenn respectively) and bootstrapped the company after realizing they felt empty after late-night doomscrolling. They're not trying to make [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) that maximizes engagement. They want to make [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) that actually works for learning.

## A Different Kind of Scroll

Here's where it gets interesting. A short video can spark curiosity. But instead of being trapped in algorithmic hell, that curiosity channels into deeper learning. "Following that curiosity can mean searching for another explanation or leaving the app. We wanted going deeper to be as easy as moving to the next video," Gupta explained.

The freemium model targets three groups: individual learners who want something better to scroll, educational institutions, and corporate training programs. Schools and companies pay annual licenses for institutional access, getting engagement metrics and progress tracking in return. Consumers get a free tier with a paid ScrollEd Pro option.

## What's Next for the Startup

The rollout timeline is aggressive. ScrollEd is launching its consumer business at Disrupt in mid-October, while simultaneously building out lesson libraries, source-checking workflows, and pilots with institutions. Down the line, they're working on adaptive learning infrastructure that could personalize content based on individual learner data.

"The ambition is a social network that's better for you," Gupta said. Not one that exploits your attention or sells your data to the highest bidder. One that actually respects how your brain works.

It's a bold swing in a landscape where edtech has mostly failed to move the needle. But ScrollEd's insight isn't wrong: if TikTok can make 15-year-olds spend five hours a day scrolling, why can't the same mechanics work for calculus textbooks?

The real question is whether they can execute without sliding into the same engagement-maximization trap that made social media toxic in the first place. That deliberate restraint will be harder than building the product.
