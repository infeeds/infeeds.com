Miranda Lambert continues her remarkable streak of chart dominance with her latest album Crisco, which debuts at No. 4 on Billboard's Top Country Albums chart. The achievement marks yet another chapter in one of country music's most impressive commercial runs: all 11 of Lambert's chart entries have landed in the top 10, a feat that dates back to her 2005 debut Kerosene.

The album, which earned 24,000 equivalent units in the U.S. during October 2-8, represents a bold creative departure for the Texas native. Produced by Lambert and Jesse Frasure, Crisco blends country and disco sounds inspired by the groovy 1970s era. The 12-track set emerged from Lambert's collaboration with Chris Stapleton on "A Song to Sing," a duet inspired by the legendary partnership of Dolly Parton and Kenny Rogers. Lambert even wears earrings that once belonged to Parton in the song's music video, while Stapleton sports a necklace from Rogers' collection. It's the first time Lambert has recorded with strings, adding orchestral texture to her signature sound.

## A New Creative Direction

Crisco kicks off with "Snakeskin Boots," a collaboration with rising artist Ella Langley that currently sits at No. 12 on Hot Country Songs. The track showcases Lambert's knack for imaginative storytelling. She explained to Billboard that she and her co-writers envisioned what would have happened if Thelma and Louise survived their famous cliff dive. The song brought together Lambert, Frasure, Natalie Hemby, and her husband Brendan McLoughlin as co-writers, marking McLoughlin's first Billboard chart credit.

The Lambert-Langley partnership has already proven successful. The duo previously collaborated on "Choosin' Texas," which recently spent its 44th week atop Hot Country Songs, marking the third-longest-leading hit in the chart's history. ## Rising Stars and Returning Artists

Lambert's dominance on the country charts hasn't left much room for others to break through, but this week brought notable debuts from other artists. Georgia native Gavin Adcock scored his second Top Country Albums top 10 entry with The Day I Hang It Up, debuting at No. 10 with 18,000 units. The 25-song collection represents significant growth for Adcock, who previously reached No. 4 with Own Worst Enemy in August 2025. Two tracks from the new album debuted on Hot Country Songs: "Workin' Man" at No. 38 and "Us Again" featuring Treaty Oak Revival at No. 45.

Meanwhile, Wynonna made her long-awaited return to the charts with The Hard Truth, which debuted at No. 45 with 8,000 units. This marks her first studio album in a decade and her 13th solo entry on the Top Country Albums chart. The intimate project was recorded at her home studio in Leiper's Fork, Tennessee, and produced by her husband Cactus Moser. The album follows Wynonna & The Big Noise, which reached No. 14 in 2016.

Wynonna's catalog speaks volumes about her staying power in country music. Her self-titled debut spent three weeks at No. 1 in 1992, the first of her three chart leaders. Her return, however modest the chart position, carries weight for longtime fans eager to hear new material from the legendary artist.

## Looking at Lambert's Legacy

Lambert's continued success on the charts remains virtually unmatched in modern country music. Crisco follows her September 2024 release Postcards from Texas, which debuted at No. 8, and Palomino from 2022 that peaked at No. 2. Her longest-charting album remains Platinum, which held the top spot for six weeks in 2014. Her other No. 1 albums include Wildcard (2019), The Weight of These Wings (2016), Four the Record (2011), Revolution (2009), Crazy Ex-Girlfriend (2007), and her 2005 debut. What's particularly striking about Lambert's success isn't just the frequency of hits but the artistic risks she continues to take. The disco-country fusion of Crisco could've been gimmicky in less capable hands, yet it demonstrates her willingness to evolve while maintaining her core identity. As the industry watches whether her perfect top-10 streak continues unbroken, one has to wonder what creative boundaries she'll push next.
