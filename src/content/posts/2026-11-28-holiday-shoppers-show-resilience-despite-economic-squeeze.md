The holiday shopping season is shaping up to be surprisingly robust, even as consumers navigate one of the toughest economic environments in years. While inflation, wage stagnation, and job market uncertainty have made Americans think twice about their wallets, the latest data tells a different story when it comes to seasonal spending.

Retailers are reporting optimism across the board. Walmart CFO John David Rainey recently told CNBC that payrolls remain healthy and the equity markets are holding steady despite geopolitical concerns. "Even though consumer sentiment is somewhat lower, it hasn't translated into reduced economic output," he said. This cautious confidence extends across the [business](https://infeeds.com/tags/business) landscape, from Target to Best Buy to Lowe's.

## The Numbers Point Upward

Planned spending on gifts and nongifts is estimated to average $1,049 this year, up 5.9% from $990 last year. When adjusted for inflation, that's still a healthy 3.2% increase year-over-year. Holiday sales overall are expected to top $1 trillion, with online spending showing particular strength. Adobe data found that consumers spent $82.5 billion online in the first 22 days of November alone, marking a 9.1% year-over-year bump.

The Black Friday and Cyber Monday week is expected to be record-breaking. Salesforce predicts online sales will reach $84 billion in the U.S. during the November 24-30 period, representing a 6% increase compared to last year. Notably, 87% of shoppers plan to participate in Black Friday and Cyber Monday sales, the highest participation rate since 2016.

## Smart Shopping and Strategic Deals

What's driving this spending isn't blind consumerism. Shoppers are being deliberately strategic about where they put their money. Retailers are offering bigger markdowns than last year, with discount rates up 8% in November alone. Consumers are hunting for value, comparing prices more carefully, and deferring big purchases until promotional windows arrive.

Generation Z is particularly engaged this season, fully embracing the deal-hunting mentality. About 40% of Gen Z shoppers plan to use buy now, pay later services, extending payments rather than depleting savings immediately. As Deloitte's Brian McCarthy explained, it's about "the mix of feeling the squeeze on prices while still wanting to protect the holidays."

## The Weather Factor

Some retailers faced unexpected headwinds early in the quarter. Warmer weather at the beginning of the fourth quarter slowed sales for Gap Inc., TJX Companies, and Burlington Stores, with some lowering guidance as a result. Gap CEO Richard Dickson acknowledged the slowdown but expressed optimism about colder weather driving renewed spending during peak holiday weeks.

That's where pent-up demand comes in. When consumers defer purchases because of weather or pricing concerns, they're not necessarily abandoning plans. They're just timing their buys strategically. CEO executives at retailers from Target to Best Buy to TJX are banking on this concentrated spending during the most important retail weeks of the year.

## Real Resilience, Not Complacency

The picture emerging isn't one of unbridled optimism or irresponsible spending. Consumers are squeezed but determined. They're prioritizing gifts and holiday experiences while cutting back on other discretionary items. Best Buy CEO Jason Bonfig noted an interesting dynamic: while shoppers emphasize value, they're also willing to upgrade into more premium price points when it matters to them.

Retailers have adapted accordingly. Target is pricing thousands of products at $5 and under. Walmart is leaning into rollbacks and value positioning. The strategy is clear: give consumers the savings they're seeking, and they'll spend on the holidays.

Macroeconomic concerns haven't disappeared. The Consumer Confidence Index hit its lowest point since 2014 in September. October's retail sales growth was the slowest in nearly a year. But these headwinds haven't stopped consumers from planning meaningful holiday expenditures. They've simply made shoppers more intentional about timing, more demanding about value, and more willing to use financing tools to manage cash flow.

The question isn't whether consumers will spend this holiday season. Early data suggests they will. The real question is whether this spending surge is sustainable or just borrowed from an uncertain future.
