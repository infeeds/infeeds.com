After weeks of anxiety and bare salad bars across the country, there's finally some good news. Cyclosporiasis cases are slowing down, and health officials are lifting warnings about lettuce consumption. But should you really feel comfortable ordering that Caesar salad again?

## The Outbreak Is Cooling Down

This summer's cyclosporiasis outbreak sparked genuine concern. Thousands of people got sick from a parasitic infection spread through contaminated fresh produce, particularly iceberg lettuce from Taylor Farms de Mexico. Michigan was hit hardest, but cases appeared across multiple states.

Now, the Michigan Department of Health and Human Services has dropped its lettuce warning, signaling that the worst may be behind us. "The number of cases is only increasing slightly, so the thought is that we're maybe past the worst of the outbreak," explained Dr. Rebecca Schein from Michigan State University's College of Human Medicine.

But here's the catch: just because cases are plateauing doesn't mean zero risk exists. Some people may have been treated without being tested, meaning actual case numbers could be slightly higher than reported. Still, experts agree the immediate danger has substantially decreased.

## What You Actually Need to Do

The simple answer is yes, salads are safe again. But with conditions.

First, throw away any recalled Taylor Farms lettuce. Second, and this cannot be overstated: wash your lettuce. Even if it says "pre-washed" on the bag. "The biggest recommendation was to avoid lettuce that comes pre-packaged in bags or boxes, because that kind of environment allows bacteria and parasites to multiply," Schein noted.

Washing alone won't guarantee removal of cyclospora once produce is contaminated, which is why recalls matter so much. However, following official recalls rather than avoiding entire food categories is the smarter approach. "Pay attention to recalls, not rumors," said Dr. Katrine Wallace, an epidemiologist at the University of Illinois Chicago.

## Beyond Lettuce: Other Food Threats

Cyclosporiasis isn't the only foodborne illness circulating right now. A salmonella outbreak linked to jalapeños is affecting multiple states, and an E. coli outbreak has been traced to frozen blueberries. These reminders underscore why general produce safety matters year-round, not just during outbreaks.

The good news? Basic hygiene practices protect against most threats. Wash your hands before preparing food and after using the bathroom. Clean countertops with antiseptic wipes, not just water, especially after contact with raw meat or eggs. Wash all fruits and vegetables regardless of where you buy them.

Produces with lots of folds, like leafy greens, raspberries, and herbs, need extra attention. Washing them multiple times is a solid strategy.

## The Real Story Here

What happened this summer actually demonstrates public health working as intended. Officials identified contaminated products, recalls were issued, exposures declined, and cases dropped. It's a textbook outbreak response, even if it felt chaotic while living through it.

"There wasn't ever a recommendation to avoid all produce," Schein emphasized. Lettuce, spinach, and other greens contain essential nutrients your body needs. Cutting them out completely isn't healthy or sustainable. Unless your doctor specifically advises otherwise, salads are back on the menu.

The takeaway isn't paranoia but preparedness. Follow [health](https://infeeds.com/tags/health) department recalls, maintain basic food safety habits, and seek medical attention if you develop persistent diarrheal illness after eating fresh produce. Don't avoid entire food categories based on fear or rumors.

"Consumers are better served by following official recall notices than by avoiding an entire food category," Wallace repeated, because it bears repeating.

Produced fresh vegetables remain one of humanity's greatest nutritional assets, and one summer outbreak shouldn't change that. Stay informed, stay cautious, stay healthy, and yes, enjoy your salad.
