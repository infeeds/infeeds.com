The walls of 9 Dundas Lane in Melbourne's Albert Park have witnessed decades of music history. Now, after nearly half a century as the beating heart of Mushroom Group, this former biscuit factory is officially on the market, marking the end of an era for one of Australia's most influential independent music companies.

When Michael Gudinski founded Mushroom Group in 1972, few could have predicted it would become a global powerhouse shaping the careers of countless artists. The company moved to Dundas Lane in 1980, and the building quickly became more than just office space. It transformed into a cultural institution, a place where deals were made and dreams were launched.

## A Landmark of Australian Music

The list of artists who've walked through Dundas Lane reads like a who's who of Australian music royalty. Kylie Minogue, Jimmy Barnes, Paul Kelly, Yothu Yindi, and Vance Joy all signed pivotal deals within its walls. International acts like Taylor Swift, The Rolling Stones, and Bruce Springsteen have connections to the building through Frontier Touring, the legendary promotion company that calls the space home.

It's easy to understand why Matt Gudinski, Michael's son and current CEO, describes leaving as bittersweet. "I've been coming here my entire life," he said in a statement. "It's hard to overstate what this place means to everyone at Mushroom." The building has become inseparable from Australia's [entertainment](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment) legacy, a physical manifestation of independent music's power and reach.

## What Makes the Space Special

The property itself is impressive: a 525-square-meter freehold corner landholding with three street frontages. The approximately 1,080 square meters of building space combines warehouse-scale proportions with character touches, including a rooftop terrace. Roughly 490 square meters occupy each of the two main levels, with an additional 100 square meters of upper-level offices.

But the real value isn't in the square footage or the architectural bones. It's in the stories embedded in those walls. Countless iconic Australian [music](https://infeeds.com/c/music) moments happened here. Negotiations that shaped careers. Conversations that changed the industry. That intangible magic is what any potential buyer would be acquiring.

## Moving Forward

Mushroom Group isn't abandoning Melbourne's music scene. The company has already announced a move to a standalone warehouse on Little Collins Street in the city's East End. The new space will house all of Mushroom's operations, including international and domestic touring, record labels, talent management, event curation, music publishing, film production, and more.

It's a strategic evolution. While Dundas Lane became a symbol, the real work of Mushroom Group has always extended far beyond one building. The company celebrated its 50th anniversary in 2023 with the documentary "Ego, The Michael Gudinski Story" and an all-star covers collection, affirming its status as a pillar of Australian music.

## The Search for a Custodian

Mushroom Group has appointed Marshall White, Stonebridge, and Advise Transact to represent the sale. Importantly, the company isn't just looking for the highest bidder. According to a statement, they're seeking "the right custodian to carry Dundas Lane's storied history into its next chapter." That suggests they care deeply about who takes over this iconic address.

Will the new owner preserve the building's music industry heritage, or will it be transformed into something entirely different? That uncertainty adds poignancy to the sale. The building's future is now in flux, awaiting a new chapter that hasn't been written yet.
