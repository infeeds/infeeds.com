If you're wrestling with your next big decision, Boston might be calling. TechCrunch's Founder Summit is happening November 4 at SoWa Power Station, and the early-bird discounts vanish this Friday, October 16. That's a hard deadline worth paying attention to.

The premise is simple but powerful: bring your toughest questions and spend a day surrounded by people who've already asked them. With 1,000 founders and investors expected, this isn't your typical conference where you passively absorb slides. This is about stress-testing your assumptions before they cost you real money.

## Making Your Next Move Count

Building a startup means making choices that echo forward. Should you raise before you're comfortable? When does hiring someone become essential? How do you know if your product actually fits the market? These aren't rhetorical questions. They're the kind that keep founders up at night, and they're exactly what the Founder Summit agenda tackles.

The speakers bring serious credentials. Kent Bennett from Bessemer Venture Partners, Lior Div of 7AI, and Vineet Edupuganti of Cogent Security aren't there to sell you anything. They're there to share what they've learned from being on both sides of the table. The real value, though, comes in the breakout sessions and roundtables where you can actually ask follow-up questions tailored to your situation.

## Pricing Before It's Gone

Founder Passes are $199 right now. Investor Passes are $399. Both include deep-dive roundtables, breakout sessions, and networking opportunities designed to be more than just surface-level handshakes. You get 1:1 meetings and small-group conversations where real connections happen. After Friday, those prices go back up.

If you're bringing someone else to [Disrupt](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech), you already know TechCrunch events aren't just about attendance. They're about momentum. There's a reason the company offers 50% discounts on second passes. The startup ecosystem thrives when people compare notes, challenge each other's thinking, and leave with new perspectives on their problems.

## Beyond the Slides

Here's what matters: you'll sit across from founders working through the exact same scaling questions. You'll hear from investors about what actually convinces them to write checks. You'll get practical guidance on fundraising, hiring, product-market fit, and building AI-native companies. But more importantly, you'll come back to work with a sharper sense of which assumptions need testing and where your plans need strengthening.

The networking piece deserves its own mention. In a room of 1,000 ambitious people, you're bound to find someone solving a complementary problem, someone ahead of you on the journey you're starting, or someone who'll care about your progress months from now. Those conversations don't happen at scale anywhere else.

The summit brings together founders at different stages, which creates natural mentorship loops. You can ask veterans what you should have learned by now. You can share what you've figured out that might help someone earlier in their journey. That reciprocal learning environment is worth the flight to Boston alone.

## The Clock is Ticking

Friday, October 16 is the real deadline. After that, the discount disappears and you're looking at full price. If Founder Summit was already on your radar, this is your moment to commit. If it wasn't, maybe it should be. Consider what decision is actually keeping you stuck right now, who in that room might help you unstick it, and whether spending a day around people solving version 2.0 of your problems could clarify your path forward.

Startups move fast, but the best ones move with intention. Taking time to question your approach might feel like stepping off the gas, but it could be the most valuable investment you make.
