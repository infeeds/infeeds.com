Sonos is making a serious push to win back user trust after last year's catastrophic app redesign. The company just announced a major app update packed with new features, including early access to its long-awaited AI voice assistant. If you've been frustrated with Sonos over the past year, this update might actually give you reason to care again.

The headliner here is the new voice assistant. US users can sign up for the waitlist starting November 6th, with actual access beginning November 12th. This isn't just some basic voice control system either. Sonos is promising natural language processing that can handle complex requests like "Play the album with a sardine tin on the cover" or "Who was the singer at the superbowl? Play their latest album on Hallway." Those are genuinely interesting capabilities that go beyond the typical "play this song" voice commands we're used to.

## What You Can Actually Do With the New Assistant

Once you get access, the voice assistant can handle the expected tasks: starting music, moving playback between rooms, grouping and ungrouping speakers, and setting timers. But the more sophisticated natural language requests are where things get interesting. It's the kind of feature that could make voice control actually useful rather than just a gimmick.

Sonos also plans to let users create up to 10 custom AI agents, which could open up some creative possibilities down the line. Imagine agents tailored for different scenarios or preferences. That's still on the roadmap, but it shows the company is thinking bigger than just a basic voice assistant.

## New App Features Beyond Voice

Beyond the voice assistant, this update brings some genuinely useful improvements to the Sonos app itself. There's a new Presets feature that lets you create shortcuts for specific playback scenarios. Need to play music at full volume in the living room? Create a preset for it. Want to play ambient music quietly in the bedroom? Another preset. These appear right on the homescreen for quick access, which is a huge quality-of-life improvement.

The Now Playing screen and mini player now support swiping to skip tracks, and you can rearrange your favorites more easily. On iOS, the System Health feature can help diagnose issues with your setup, which is particularly helpful if you've got a complex multi-room system.

These might sound like small tweaks, but they're exactly the kind of thoughtful improvements users have been asking for. The [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) industry is full of companies that ignore user feedback for years. Sonos is at least trying to listen.

## Recovery Mode After a Disaster

Context matters here. Sonos launched a completely redesigned app in 2024 that was an absolute dumpster fire. Features disappeared, the interface confused longtime users, and the company's CEO Tom Conrad promised to undo the damage. These updates are part of that recovery effort.

The September rollout of the Sonos 27 app operating system already signaled the company was taking things seriously. Now with voice assistant access and these quality-of-life improvements, Sonos is actually backing up those promises. That's worth noting in an industry where companies often just hope people forget about their failures.

There's still a long way to go in rebuilding user confidence, but at least Sonos isn't pretending the problem didn't exist. The voice assistant launch combined with these other features suggests the company might actually get its act together.

Will these updates be enough to win back angry customers? That depends on whether Sonos can execute flawlessly going forward. One solid update doesn't erase months of frustration, but it's definitely a step in the right direction. If you've been on the fence about Sonos, this might be worth paying attention to. And if you're already a customer, these improvements might finally make your system feel like it's worth the premium you paid for it.
