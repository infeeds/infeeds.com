Brazilian distributor H2O Films is making bold moves to champion local cinema on both the documentary and narrative fronts. The company will theatrically release Miguel Faria Jr.'s documentary 'Tom Jobim - Esse Seu Olhar' across Brazil starting the week of January 25, marking the centennial celebration of the legendary bossa nova composer who created the iconic 'The Girl From Ipanema' before his death in 1994.

Simultaneously, H2O is bringing the narrative feature 'Seduction' to the world premiere stage, with the film set to debut on September 11 at the 30th edition of the Inffinito Brazilian Film Festival in Miami. This dual release strategy demonstrates the distributor's commitment to elevating Brazilian storytelling across multiple formats and audiences.

## Tom Jobim's Legacy Through His Own Voice

Faria Jr. has established himself as the go-to documentarian for Brazilian music icons. His previous work includes the critically acclaimed 'Vinicius' (2005) about Vinicius de Moraes and 'Chico: Artista Brasileiro' (2015) centered on Chico Buarque. Both films resonated strongly with audiences, proving there's genuine appetite for intimate portraits of Brazil's musical treasures.

The Tom Jobim doc takes an innovative approach. Rather than rely solely on talking heads and archival footage, Faria Jr. conducted extensive research to gather interviews with Jobim himself from television and other media sources. He then edited these materials so that 70 to 80 percent of the documentary is narrated directly by Tom Jobim. It's a clever strategy that lets the man speak for himself, lending authenticity and intimacy that few documentaries achieve.

The production brings together notable Brazilian creative talent, with Diana Vasconcellos co-writing the screenplay alongside Faria Jr. and serving as editor. The film is an Arpoador Audiovisual and Urca Filmes production, co-produced by Globo Filmes, with producers Jorge Peregrino and Leonardo Edde steering the ship.

## A Director's Debut in the Amazon

Meanwhile, 'Seduction' represents something quite different: the directorial debut of Marcos Palmeira, an actor who has anchored dozens of telenovelas, series, and films over a 50-year career in Brazilian [entertainment](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment). He doesn't go it alone, though. Palmeira co-directs with his father Zelito Viana, a veteran producer of Cinema Novo classics and director of features including 'Villa-Lobos: A Life of Passion.'

The film follows Paulo (Palmeira), a renowned actor who journeys into the Amazon Rainforest after learning of his father's death. What unfolds is a story of settling unfinished affairs, confronting harsh regional realities, and undergoing profound personal transformation. Shot in Alter do Chao along the Tapajos River, the production grapples with pressing local issues including illegal land grabbing.

Peregrino, speaking with industry publications, praised the collaboration between father and son. He declared it Viana's finest work to date, even surpassing 'Villa-Lobos: A Life of Passion.' He also credited Palmeira's direction with bringing freshness and vitality to the narrative.

## High Production Value Meets Social Commentary

The film boasts impressive production values and features two of Brazil's biggest screen names: Camila Morgado as Sara Teixeira and Dira Paes as Maria Rita Martins. This caliber of cast, combined with the Amazon setting and thematic depth, positions 'Seduction' as a major release for festival circuit prestige.

The Inffinito Brazilian Film Festival, where 'Seduction' will premiere, is the largest Brazilian film festival held abroad. It operates as a crucial window for international distribution, with screenings across Miami, New York City, London, Vancouver, Rome, Milan, Madrid, Barcelona, Buenos Aires, and Shanghai. For emerging filmmakers and established productions alike, Inffinito represents a genuine opportunity to reach global [audiences](https://infeeds.com/tags/film-festivals).

H2O Films' dual commitment to both the Tom Jobim documentary and Palmeira's directorial debut signals confidence in Brazilian cinema's ability to captivate viewers worldwide. One offers a intimate conversation with a musical icon; the other presents a transformative journey through one of Earth's most challenging and contested landscapes. Together, they represent the diversity and depth of contemporary Brazilian storytelling.
