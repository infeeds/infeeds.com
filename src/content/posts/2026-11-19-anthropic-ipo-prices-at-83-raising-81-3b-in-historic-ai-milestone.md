Anthropic, the artificial intelligence company behind the popular Claude chatbot, priced its highly anticipated IPO at $83 per share Wednesday, exceeding initial expectations and cementing a watershed moment for the AI industry. The company and its seven co-founders upsized the offering to approximately 980 million shares, raising a staggering $81.3 billion and surpassing SpaceX's previous record IPO from June.

The valuation represents a fully diluted market cap of around $1.5 trillion for the company that was founded just six years ago by researchers who departed OpenAI. Stock trading under the ticker "ANT" is set to begin Thursday on the Nasdaq, drawing intense attention from investors betting on AI's transformative potential.

## Record-Breaking Numbers in a Volatile Market

The IPO success is particularly striking given the turbulent landscape surrounding artificial intelligence. The sector has faced mounting skepticism following a dramatic political backlash in recent midterm elections and last week's shocking ouster of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. Market jitters have intensified over concerns that rising interest rates will make debt prohibitively expensive for data center developers and emerging neocloud companies.

Yet investors appear undeterred by these headwinds. The company had initially planned to sell 825 million shares in a price range of $70 to $78 per share when it unveiled its prospectus in October. The decision to upsize and price above that range demonstrates robust demand from institutional buyers and signals confidence in Anthropic's business model and growth trajectory.

Each of Anthropic's seven co-founders is selling 3.38 million shares, netting approximately $1.96 billion in total proceeds from the offering. It's a significant payday for the founding team, particularly CEO Dario Amodei, who led the group's departure from OpenAI to launch Anthropic in 2021.

## Explosive Growth and Ambitious Revenue Targets

Anthropric's financial metrics paint a picture of hypergrowth that justifies the astronomical valuation. For the nine-month period ending September 30, the company generated $33.24 billion in revenue, representing roughly 1,200 percent growth compared to the same period last year. Even more impressive, Anthropic disclosed that its run-rate revenue reached approximately $81 billion by late October, up $10 billion from September alone.

The company's financial story is more nuanced than headline revenue figures suggest. While Anthropic reported a $104 billion net loss during the nine-month period, the majority stemmed from changes in fair value of convertible notes and derivative liabilities rather than operational losses. The actual operating loss of $25.1 billion in 2025 remains substantial but reflects the capital-intensive nature of [AI development](https://infeeds.com/tags/ai) and infrastructure buildout.

## Deep-Pocketed Partners and Strategic Support

Anthropic's IPO success owes much to backing from technology's heaviest hitters. Amazon and Google each own stakes valued in excess of $100 billion. A consortium including Google, Microsoft, Nvidia, and Advanced Micro Devices collectively committed to investing $26 billion at the IPO price, providing crucial validation and capital support.

This interconnected ecosystem reflects both opportunity and risk. Anthropic depends on these same companies for expensive computing infrastructure, creating potential conflicts of interest and supply chain vulnerabilities. At the same time, major tech companies' significant investments suggest they view Anthropic's Claude models as essential to competing in enterprise AI markets.

The company has been racing against OpenAI and Google to maintain leadership in the enterprise sector, with impressive gains in 2026 driven partly by Claude Code, its popular AI coding tool. OpenAI, meanwhile, has confidentially filed for its own IPO, with a debut likely in 2027.

## Safety and Scrutiny

Anthropric's rapid rise hasn't come without controversy. The company has engaged in two high-profile disputes with the U.S. government this year over positions regarding safety, regulation, and national security. The prospectus includes over 90 pages of risk factors, signaling the regulatory and geopolitical complexities surrounding advanced AI development.

CEO Amodei framed the mission in the company's roadshow video: "I think if we do this right, we can create an age of miracles where humanity is able to solve problems that have eluded us for millennia." But he acknowledged the imperative to carefully address technological risks while building a viable business.

The success of Anthropic's IPO will test whether investors remain bullish on AI despite mounting questions about regulation, competition, and real-world profitability. Will this be the first of many monster AI IPOs, or a peak before skepticism takes hold?
