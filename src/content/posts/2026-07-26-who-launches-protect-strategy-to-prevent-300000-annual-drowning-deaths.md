Nearly 300,000 people die from drowning every year, yet most of these deaths are entirely preventable. The World Health Organization is taking action ahead of World Drowning Prevention Day on July 25 by launching the PROTECT technical package, a comprehensive framework designed to help governments and communities implement proven drowning prevention strategies.

The scale of the problem is staggering. Drowning ranks among the ten leading causes of death for children aged 5 to 14 years worldwide, with the vast majority of incidents occurring in rivers, lakes, wells, domestic water storage containers, and swimming pools. What's particularly troubling is that more than 90% of these deaths happen in low- and middle-income countries, where resources for prevention and water safety education are often severely limited.

## Climate Change Is Making Drowning Worse

Climate change is intensifying the crisis. Extreme weather events like floods, storms, and heatwaves are becoming more frequent, directly increasing drowning risks. The correlation between heat and water deaths is especially striking. Research from the United Kingdom found that drowning deaths increase by 7.2% for every 1-degree Celsius rise in temperature. On days when temperatures exceed 25°C, the risk is nearly five times higher compared with days below 10°C. As global temperatures continue rising, we can expect drowning rates to climb unless preventive action accelerates.

Ghana, where an estimated 1,100 people die from drowning annually, exemplifies the urgency. That's approximately three preventable deaths every single day. The WHO chose Ghana for the PROTECT package launch specifically to recognize the country's growing political commitment to addressing this crisis and its efforts to strengthen water safety infrastructure.

## Seven Strategies for Real Change

The PROTECT framework provides policymakers with concrete, evidence-informed recommendations across seven interconnected domains to strengthen safety around water. The acronym represents specific interventions that have proven effective in reducing drowning deaths. These aren't theoretical concepts but practical approaches that governments can implement immediately.

Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, WHO Director-General, emphasized that drowning prevention requires collective action: "Every drowning is a tragedy, and most are preventable. The seven PROTECT strategies give countries practical, evidence-based approaches to prevent these tragedies, including making water transport safer, teaching school kids to swim, and preparing for floods."

What makes this framework particularly valuable is its recognition that drowning prevention cannot be the responsibility of a single ministry. Education, transportation, urban planning, emergency response, and healthcare all play critical roles. When these sectors work in coordination, the impact multiplies. Schools teaching swimming, infrastructure improvements ensuring safer water transport, and communities prepared for flood events all contribute to reducing deaths.

## Building on Global Momentum

This technical package builds on more than a decade of collaboration between WHO, governments, and partner organizations. Bloomberg Philanthropies, which has supported drowning prevention efforts since 2014, views PROTECT as an essential tool for scaling proven interventions worldwide.

Dr Kelly Henning, who leads Bloomberg Philanthropies' public health program, noted: "This framework will save lives while building on the progress governments around the world are making to prevent drowning deaths in their countries."

The timing matters. This year's World Drowning Prevention Day theme, "Unite to turn the tide," underscores the need for coordinated action across governments, sectors, and communities. It's a call to recognize that drowning prevention requires everyone's involvement and commitment. Policymakers must prioritize water safety education in schools. Urban planners must design safer water infrastructure. Healthcare systems must be prepared for water-related emergencies. Communities must engage in prevention efforts at the local level.

The PROTECT technical package transforms the first-ever Global Strategy for Drowning Prevention into actionable policy guidance. It's no longer acceptable to treat drowning deaths as inevitable. With these seven proven measures now packaged and ready for implementation, every government has access to evidence-based solutions that work. The question is whether they'll have the political will to implement them before another 300,000 people lose their lives to a preventable tragedy.
