## Devastating Floods Strike Nepal-Tibet Border

The death toll from catastrophic flash floods at the Nepal-Tibet border has climbed past 460 people, with more than 1,500 individuals still missing. Among those unaccounted for are approximately 90 Americans, according to the U.S. State Department.

The U.S. Geological Survey has determined that a glacial collapse triggered the devastating floods. In response, the State Department announced it would contribute $500,000 in aid to support rescue efforts in the region. The tragedy underscores the vulnerability of border communities to sudden environmental disasters, and rescue teams continue searching for survivors amid the debris.

## Fed Chair Warsh Takes the Stage on Inflation

Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh is delivering his first major address as Fed Chair this morning at Jackson Lake Lodge in Wyoming, speaking to economists and policymakers gathered for what has become a closely watched annual event.

Investors are hoping for answers. Last month, Warsh promised action to curb inflation but left many disappointed by the lack of specific details. The central bank's target inflation rate remains elusive, with prices climbing 3.7% in the 12 months ending in July. Historically, Fed chairs use this Wyoming gathering as a platform to articulate their economic philosophy and provide guidance that moves markets. The pressure on Warsh to deliver clarity is palpable.

## Kennedy Center Name Battle Heats Up in Court

A federal judge heard arguments concerning the Kennedy Center board's push to reinstate President Trump's name at the arts complex's entrance. On August 13, the board voted to close the main building for renovations and install new signage reading "The John F. Kennedy Memorial Center for the Performing Arts, Restored and Renovated by President Donald J. Trump."

The board, dominated by Trump appointees, made headlines further this week when it suggested in legal filings that the complex could face demolition if its two-year renovation plans, including Trump's name restoration, don't move forward. The decision has sparked considerable debate about presidential honors, corporate naming rights, and the purpose of public cultural institutions. This case could set important precedent for how public buildings memorialize political figures.

## Trump Pushes Ground Beef Import Plan

President Trump announced a plan to temporarily lower tariff rates on imported ground beef in an attempt to ease pressure on American consumers facing record prices at the butcher counter.

In a social media post, Trump stated that the U.S. would "allow up to 300,000 metric tons of product for ground beef to be imported with no out of quota tariff" and pledged to sell this beef at "25 percent below market prices." Last month, U.S. consumers faced an average price of $6.89 per pound for ground beef. However, agricultural industry experts express skepticism about whether the plan will deliver meaningful price relief to shoppers.
