Apple's new Apple TV 4K costs $299, which is a hefty price for a black box that mostly sits idle under your television. But the real question isn't what you're paying today, it's how long before you'll feel pressured to buy the next one. Unfortunately, Apple keeps this answer deliberately vague.

Unlike Amazon, which promises at least four years of security updates for Fire TV devices, Apple doesn't publicly commit to any specific support timeline for tvOS. There's no guarantee printed anywhere. That said, Apple's track record tells a much more optimistic story than you might expect.

## A Decade of Updates (Sometimes)

The 2015 Apple TV HD and the original 2017 Apple TV 4K both received major software updates all the way through tvOS 26 in 2025. That's ten years and eight years of support respectively, with security patches continuing into 2026. Pretty remarkable for hardware that was originally designed to last maybe half that long.

But here's where things get complicated. In September 2026, Apple released tvOS 27, and suddenly the older models couldn't upgrade. The compatibility list started fresh with the second-generation Apple TV 4K from 2021. The 2015 and 2017 models are now stuck, and Apple hasn't even confirmed whether they'll receive additional security patches going forward.

Netflix still works fine on these older devices, so they're not suddenly worthless. But as developers gradually raise their minimum software requirements, apps will eventually stop working or refuse to update. It's a slow fade to obsolescence rather than a cliff edge.

## Performance Matters More Than Age

Here's the thing: your Apple TV doesn't become an expensive brick the moment it can't install the latest OS. The real question is whether it still performs well enough for your needs. When menus start lagging, apps crash frequently, or services you actually use stop working, that's when an upgrade makes practical sense.

Security is worth considering too, especially if you've configured your Apple TV as a smart home hub. Older devices without security patches become potential vulnerabilities in your home network, which adds another reason to eventually upgrade [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) infrastructure.

## The $299 Question

The new Apple TV 4K does have legitimate improvements: the A19 processor is noticeably faster, and built-in microphones enable hands-free Siri control without needing a remote. The Siri AI beta will actually work on older models too, so you're not forced to buy the latest hardware just for that feature.

But $299 is genuinely expensive for an incremental upgrade. If your current Apple TV runs smoothly and has the apps you need, there's no compelling reason to rush into this purchase. The 2022 model works perfectly fine for most people.

## Protecting Your Investment

One smart move: if your Siri Remote gets lost or damaged, don't automatically replace your entire setup. Apple's latest Siri Remote is backward compatible with all Apple TV 4K models and even the 2015 Apple TV HD. A replacement remote costs $59, which is far more reasonable than a full device replacement. You can also use your iPhone as a remote in a pinch.

The reality is that Apple's support timeline remains a mystery, and the company clearly prefers it that way. But based on the past decade of support for older models, you can reasonably expect six to eight years of major updates from the new Apple TV 4K, possibly extending to ten. Security patches might continue beyond that.

The best strategy is to ignore the upgrade cycle entirely and focus on actual performance and functionality. Keep your Apple TV as long as it works. Only upgrade when you genuinely need new features or when aging hardware starts failing you, not because a calendar year changed or a new processor arrived. After all, how much faster can a device need to be just to let you pick something to watch?
