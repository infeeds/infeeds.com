Jason Ganahl didn't plan to build a barbecue empire. He just wanted to answer his friends' questions.

When people started asking how to smoke salmon or grill asparagus, Ganahl saw an opportunity. He'd been winning barbecue competitions and knew his way around a smoker. But he also watched Greg Mrvich demonstrate techniques on Ballistic BBQ and realized something: YouTube was the perfect platform to share what he knew.

## From YouTube Tutorials to Real-World Service

Ganahl launched his channel to give people answers they couldn't find elsewhere. "YouTube was born out of, I like to just share, I like to give," he explains. "That's why the restaurant industry is great to me." The channel grew to 109,000 subscribers, though Ganahl admits he doesn't have the bandwidth to maintain it now. When he opened GQue Championship BBQ in Westminster, Colorado in November 2015, the restaurant became his primary focus.

What's striking is how Ganahl treated his initial venture into content creation. He didn't just film barbecue. He used it as a learning tool. "I thought this was a good opportunity to teach myself how to edit, create content and learn how to be pretty good at it," he says. That mindset about continuous improvement carries through everything he does.

The [business](https://infeeds.com/tags/business) of running a restaurant differs vastly from uploading videos, though both require consistency. His older videos remain online, still helping people with lobster tails or smoking techniques. But Ganahl's energy now goes into something harder: maintaining quality across multiple locations.

## The Hiring Philosophy That Scaled a Company

When demand for GQue grew, Ganahl faced a critical choice about expansion. He credits smart hiring decisions with making growth possible. He looks for people who care as much as he does about what guests experience when they walk in. "I've never been able, and I've never seen anybody, to be able to take chicken shit and turn it into chicken salad," Ganahl says bluntly.

He won't compromise on priorities. At GQue, that means smoking brisket over hickory for same-day service and keeping prices reasonable for someone buying barbecue on an ordinary day. When someone brings a friend back for the brisket, both need the same quality that earned the recommendation.

This hiring philosophy worked. GQue grew from one Westminster location to six standalone restaurants. The company also operates seven concession stands across three stadiums, including two at Folsom Field where they serve University of Colorado football fans and staff. Two more stands operate at Empower Field and three at Coors Field.

## Growing Through Genuine Connection

Ganahl's strategy for expansion remains rooted in word-of-mouth. "Ganahl wants guests to leave GQue and tell a friend where they ate. If enough people do that, the demand for another restaurant takes care of the decision about when to open one," he notes. "Then we're forced to grow and open up a new spot."

This approach reflects deeper philosophy. The goal isn't aggressive expansion for its own sake. It's creating experiences so good that people voluntarily become your marketing team. That starts with hiring the right people who understand the mission.

"It's way more fun doing it with more people than trying to do everything on your own," Ganahl says. That sentiment captures something important about [entrepreneurship](https://infeeds.com/tags/entrepreneurship) often lost in headlines about founders and fortunes. Building a company that matters means building it with people who actually care.

Ganahl started at weekend barbecue competitions. Now people serve GQue's food on his behalf at counters across Colorado. The journey from YouTube tutorials to stadium concessions happened not through aggressive scaling but through obsessive attention to whether the next person gets "the best 20 minutes of their day."
