Xbox's week got worse before it got better. The platform suffered a major outage that knocked online services offline for nearly 24 hours, but the real kicker was what happened to physical game discs. Players discovered they couldn't play their disc-based games either, which should have been impossible.

Scott Van Vliet, Xbox's technology chief, confirmed to The Verge that this wasn't supposed to happen. "We're looking into reports that some players were unable to access games using discs as expected during the service interruption," he said. "Disc-based entitlement checks should not prevent players from accessing their games and this is by design."

## The Design Flaw Nobody Expected

Here's where it gets interesting. The entire outage was triggered by problems at an external licensing service, which explains why online functionality went down. But discs? Those shouldn't need the internet at all. In theory, your console has local copies of your disc entitlements stored, allowing you to play anytime, anywhere, even in a cabin with zero connectivity.

Except it didn't work that way this week.

Van Vliet explained that the system designed to save disc entitlements locally simply failed to do its job correctly during the outage. It's a technical breakdown that raises uncomfortable questions about how tightly integrated Xbox's [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) infrastructure has become, even for physical media.

## The Timing Couldn't Be Worse

This incident arrives as Xbox is already dealing with serious headwinds. The company wrapped up a rough 2025, brought in new leadership, announced price increases, and plans to cut 3,200 jobs. Add a major service outage that breaks even offline gaming, and you've got a PR disaster stacking on top of existing frustrations.

For players who paid for physical games specifically to avoid digital dependency, this outage felt like a betrayal. They bought discs thinking they'd have reliable, always-accessible entertainment. Instead, they discovered their console couldn't verify their ownership when the servers were down.

## What Happens Next

Van Vliet promised a fix is coming in an upcoming update. He didn't specify when, but the acknowledgment that this shouldn't have happened is at least transparent. The company clearly understands the design principle: local entitlements should mean local access.

The real question is whether this fix will actually work and whether Xbox's infrastructure needs deeper changes. You can't keep relying on external licensing services for basic functionality [gaming](https://infeeds.com/tags/gaming) features that don't inherently require internet connectivity.

Physical media enthusiasts have long argued that discs provide a safeguard against corporate service shutdowns. This week proved they have a point, even if the execution failed temporarily.
