## Finding Your Perfect Fit

There are three things you shouldn't skimp on if you want a happy life: a good mattress, a good pair of shoes, and the best office chair you can find. Each is key to a world free of unnecessary pain. If you're working from home, your chair matters more than ever.

Finding the right ergonomic chair can be the difference between a satisfying and focused day and a squirmy mess filled with cricks and small dissatisfactions. The best desk chair is almost always an adjustable one that can be tailored to your body's unique shape and offers enough lumbar support to avoid back problems even after long hours at the grind.

## What Makes a Great Office Chair

Many chairs utilize materials that dissipate body heat, so you're not stuck in video calls with a sweaty back. But the chair options out there can be bewildering. The WIRED Reviews team has spent more than seven years sorting them out, testing more than 60 chairs to find the real winners.

One of the main markers of a good office chair is how well it adjusts to fit your particular shape and size. Adjustable armrests, seat depth, recline angles, and lumbar support aren't luxury features, they're essentials. People come in different sizes, which is why testing these chairs with people of varying heights and body types matters.

## Budget-Friendly Options

WIRED's favorite office chair for most people is the Branch Ergonomic Chair Pro at $499. It offers an impressive balance between value, features, comfort, and adjustability. The armrests move up and down, forward and back, and angle inward or outward. Unlike most comparable chairs, the arms can also move in and out to accommodate wider or narrower bodies.

If you're on a tighter budget, the Branch Ergonomic Chair at $369 is still a great place to start. It assembles in minutes and offers adjustable lumbar support and recline locking. While it lacks the forward tilt option of the Pro, testers found it comfortable across different heights.

For those needing something even cheaper, the Staples Dexley at $150 is miles better than most cheap chairs on Amazon. The all-mesh seat is great for airflow, though it is a little rough on the skin. You can't find much better at that price point.

## Premium Choices Worth the Investment

If you can swing it, the Steelcase Gesture at $1,510 is preternaturally comfortable no matter how you're sitting. The seat is cushy, and you can extend the seat depth forward with a twist of a knob. The recline knob smartly offers a series of clicks to indicate the angles you can lock into place.

The Herman Miller Embody is another premium option. It might take a few weeks to get used to it, but it's well worth your patience. The seat feels rigid at first but eventually becomes surprisingly pillowy. With a 12-year warranty covering every part, this chair is built to last. At around $1,465 or more, it's an investment, but many users report it feels good as new after years of daily use.

## Finding What Works for You

We all have differing body types and needs, which is why a broad collection of recommendations matters. WIRED continues to enlist people of all sizes to test top picks for comfort and durability. Whether you need a [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) upgrade for your workspace or you're just starting to build your home office setup, there's a chair for your budget and body type.

The price of a good office chair often inspires actual gasps, easily cresting $1,000. This is typically justified because most cheaper chairs reveal their shortcomings quickly and uncomfortably. If you have to sit at a desk for a living, saving up for a nice, high-end chair is worth considering. That said, buying a solid budget option now is better than suffering through an uncomfortable chair while you save.
