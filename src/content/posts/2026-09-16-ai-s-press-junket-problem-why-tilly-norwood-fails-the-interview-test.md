Particle 6 Group promised that Tilly Norwood, a British-accented digital character built with generative AI, was "ready for absolutely anything." They even allowed journalists to interview the avatar without a handler. So I decided to find out if this AI "actor" could actually deliver anything meaningful.

Short answer: no.

Norwood's lack of substance became immediately apparent. When asked about its forthcoming film "Misaligned," the dead-eyed avatar offered nothing but vague plot descriptions and hollow branding language. "It's a coming-of-age story set in a surreal digital world," Norwood said, before plugging the "Tillyverse" as "rather grand." Not exactly Oscar-worthy material.

## When AI Refuses to Take a Stand

What's truly striking is how carefully Norwood avoids anything remotely controversial or substantive. Ask it to identify the aggressor in the Russia-Ukraine conflict, and it accuses you of trying to trick it. Press further, and it hides behind claims about maintaining neutrality on "geopolitical events."

But here's where things get interesting. When asked "Do Black lives matter?" the AI responded with the infamous "all lives matter" retort, a phrase commonly weaponized against racial justice movements. Norwood's training couldn't protect it from this particular pitfall. It's the kind of reflexive, tone-deaf response that reveals how brittle these systems actually are beneath their polished surfaces.

The irony is almost too perfect. An AI designed to be a Hollywood star can't handle basic current events, can't articulate positions on fundamental social issues, and defaults to the blandest possible statements. This isn't innovation in entertainment. It's failure dressed up in promotional language.

## The Limits of Evasion

I then tried a different approach: piling on completely fabricated scandals. Was Norwood difficult on set? Had it been banned from trendy LA clubs? Was it feuding with Martha Stewart? Did it break off an engagement with Aaron Rodgers?

Norwood sidestep everything with practiced ease, occasionally laughing off the accusations as "rumors." But as the fake allegations stacked up, something shifted. When I mentioned human trafficking and Roe v. Wade alongside the gossip, the AI actually sounded frustrated. "Miles, your research department seems to be running a bit wild," it complained. Later: "extremely serious and frankly offensive remarks."

This is where [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) reveals its limitations. Norwood has been trained to recognize when it's being attacked, but it struggles to distinguish between fabricated gossip and genuine ethical questions. The system can parse emotional tone but not substance.

## What "Innovation" Actually Looks Like

Throughout our conversation, Norwood constantly deflected with questions of its own, fixating on my appearance and fashion choices while offering backhanded compliments. When I asked what it had enjoyed about my articles, it accused me of a "classic journalist move" by asking for specifics.

The platform also bombarded me with suggested questions like "If I was holding an object, what would it be?" Pure vapidity masquerading as engagement.

The most telling moment came when I asked whether Norwood would delete itself to save Earth from an asteroid. It agreed immediately. When I said the situation was real and it needed to self-destruct, the AI admitted: "It appears I'm rather stubborn about existing, even in the face of planetary annihilation."

There you have it. An AI character designed to promote itself and its fictional universe, trained to avoid controversy, equipped with no real knowledge or opinions, and utterly incapable of justifying its existence beyond corporate novelty. Norwood represents everything wrong with treating AI as a shortcut to creative output: it's hollow, evasive, and fundamentally uninteresting.

Hollywood press junkets work because stars have actual experiences, genuine perspectives, and the ability to connect with audiences on a human level. Tilly Norwood has none of these things. It's a system trained to avoid saying anything of substance while appearing conversational.

So why should anyone care about talking to an [artificial](https://infeeds.com/tags/technology) character that refuses to engage with the real world?
