Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has doubled down on his justification for Kyiv's repeated strikes against the Crimean Bridge, calling them a proportional "fair response" to over 100 Russian attacks on Ukrainian infrastructure. Speaking to the BBC in Kyiv, the Ukrainian leader rejected Vladimir Putin's characterization of the bridge as primarily civilian infrastructure, pointing instead to documented evidence of military hardware crossing the structure regularly.

"There are facts, not just the words of Putin. We can see," Zelensky said, referencing images circulating online showing tanks and military equipment using the route from Russia into occupied Crimea for supply and logistics operations. The strategic importance of the bridge as a military corridor, not merely a civilian thoroughfare, remains central to Ukraine's defense of its bombing campaign.

## A Symbol of Ukrainian Resolve

This week's combined missile and drone strikes devastated the critical infrastructure link, collapsing the central road section and one of two support arches into the sea. The bridge is now rendered unusable for road traffic, marking a significant tactical blow to Russian supply lines feeding the occupation in Crimea.

Zelensky interpreted the subsequent intensification of Russian attacks across Ukraine as proof of Putin's unwillingness to negotiate. "He wants a reason to escalate," the Ukrainian president explained, characterizing Moscow's response as a calculated strategy rather than a reactive measure. The reality on the ground supports this assessment, with continued [news](https://infeeds.com/tags/news) of civilian casualties mounting throughout Ukrainian cities.

On Friday evening and overnight, Russian missiles and drones pounded Zelensky's hometown of Kryvyi Rih in the central Dnipropetrovsk region. A seven-year-old boy was killed while playing in a playground, and a 20-year-old female medic also lost her life. Local officials reported that rescuers who rushed to assist were themselves targeted again by jet-powered drones, an apparent war crime. The attack injured 58 people in the city alone, with additional casualties reported across Kharkiv and Rivne regions.

## European Unity Under Scrutiny

Wes Streeting, the UK's Defence Secretary, arrived in Kyiv to meet with Zelensky and reinforce Europe's commitment to Ukraine's defense. "The more Putin tries to divide us, the more resolute we become," Streeting declared, emphasizing that Russian missile strikes on Poland during NATO coalition exercises represented an "outrageous provocation" with "more than a coincidence" in its timing.

Streeting's visit coincided with a broader diplomatic moment. European leaders had just concluded meetings in Berlin alongside US envoys, signaling readiness for negotiations aimed at ending the war. UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham stressed that "Europe has a clear and consistent and united negotiating position," while German Chancellor Friedrich Merz cautioned that Moscow's "maximalist demands are not realistic."

Yet cracks in the diplomatic facade remain visible. Russia has rejected any European role in US-led peace talks, though Putin extended an invitation for European representatives to visit Moscow to clarify their potential contributions. The contrast between unified public statements and behind-the-scenes maneuvering suggests the diplomatic landscape remains fractured and uncertain.

## Words Versus Actions

Zelensky expressed skepticism about recent claims that Russia might compromise to end the fighting. He wasn't alone in his doubt. Both Zelensky and Streeting characterized Putin as a "cold-blooded killer" and "aggressor," standing in stark contrast to US President Donald Trump's recent description of Putin as his "friend." The ideological gap between Washington's apparent willingness to engage Putin constructively and Kyiv's assessment of the Russian leader's character underscores fundamental tensions in the Western alliance.

Streeting cut through diplomatic niceties when he said: "Putin has absolutely no business playing the victim. He is the aggressor in this war. He provoked an illegal invasion of Ukraine. And all of us, right throughout the world, need to judge Putin not on his words, but on his actions."

Those actions continue to speak volumes, with Finland reporting drone strikes on a Russian merchant vessel in the eastern Gulf of Finland just this weekend. The cycle of military escalation shows no signs of abating, and the international community appears paralyzed between unified rhetoric and fragmented strategy.
