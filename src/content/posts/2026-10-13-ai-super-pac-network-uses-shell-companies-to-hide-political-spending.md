A shopping center in Sparks, Nevada, sits next to a Spirit Halloween and a Vietnamese restaurant. It's also home to a post office box that's become ground zero for a major campaign finance mystery. Lantern Production Consultants LLC, which appears to exist only at that address, has received roughly $20 million from pro-AI super PACs since last year.

This isn't an isolated case. It's part of a broader pattern where the Leading the Future PAC network, backed by Silicon Valley heavyweights like Marc Andreessen and OpenAI's Greg Brockman, has funneled over $140 million through at least four mysterious Nevada LLCs. The Campaign Legal Center alleges these are shell companies designed to obscure where money actually goes and who's actually receiving it.

## Following the Money Through Storefronts

Summit Ridge Media Group LLC lists its address at a mail center in a Reno strip mall. Blue Horizon Compliance Consultants operates from a Staples location. Prusik Partners LLC shares a Henderson strip mall with a chiropractor and beauty salon. None of these appear to be real operating businesses in any traditional sense.

The payments themselves are substantial. Think Big, a super PAC supporting Democrats, paid Lantern Production Consultants for polling, digital advertising, research, and strategy work. American Mission, which backs Republicans, sent $13 million to Summit Ridge Media Group between December 2025 and September 2026. This is systemic infrastructure, not a one-off quirk.

"This is about hiding where the money is going," argues Shanna Ports, senior legal counsel at the Campaign Legal Center. In May, her organization filed a Federal Election Campaign Act complaint alleging that the Leading the Future network violated disclosure requirements by using these LLCs as intermediaries. The shell companies, they wrote, "function as mere extensions of the super PACs they serve."

## Why Both Sides?

What makes this particularly notable is that Leading the Future supports both Republicans and Democrats, which breaks the typical pattern. Political consultants usually pick a lane. "If you're running in a Democrat primary and Think Big comes to spend in your race, and they drop half a million on your congressional district doing positive ads while using Republican vendors and strategists, that's news," explains Kyle Morse of the Tech Oversight Project.

That cross-partisan coordination raises uncomfortable questions about what's actually being coordinated and why vendors need protection from public association. Leading the Future spokesperson Jesse Hunt pushed back, calling the complaint a "desperate ploy for attention" from Anthropic and Public First, another pro-AI group. Public First denied involvement entirely.

## The FEC's Empty Teeth

Here's the kicker: the Federal Election Commission can't actually do anything about this right now. The FEC requires a quorum of four commissioners to decide cases, but it hasn't had one since May 2025. The [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) world has effectively called a rules timeout just as the game got interesting.

Meanwhile, the Campaign Legal Center isn't alone in flagging these tactics. They've also filed complaints against pro-gun congressional candidate Brandon Herrera for funneling 99 percent of campaign disbursements through a single vendor, and against Lead Left PAC, which allegedly used Republican operatives to meddle in Democratic primaries while posing as a Democratic entity.

This pattern reveals something unsettling about modern [politics](https://infeeds.com/tags/politics). Dark money in politics is bad enough on its own. But now we're entering an era where groups hide not just where money comes from, but where it actually goes. Voters can't trace the source or the destination.

Ports frames it bluntly: "There's no way of tracing the source behind the money, and that's bad enough on its own. Now there's this new component, where groups are playing games to hide where the money is being spent. As a result, voters really lack fundamental information about where the money is coming from and where it's going."

With the FEC essentially paralyzed and Leading the Future continuing to pour millions through these mysterious Nevada boxes, the question isn't whether this is legal or illegal anymore. The question is whether we've entered a phase of campaign finance where nobody can actually see what's happening, and whether transparency itself has become optional.
