The World Health Organization has unveiled a transformative approach to one of global health's most persistent challenges: the lack of reliable, timely health data in countries around the world. Called SCORE for Health Data Technical Package, this initiative represents the first harmonized collection of tools designed to help governments strengthen their [health information systems](https://infeeds.com/tags/health) and make better decisions based on solid evidence.

The timing couldn't be more critical. COVID-19 has exposed a painful reality: too many nations lack both the infrastructure to consistently gather health data and the capacity to analyze what they do collect. Without this information, governments fumble in the dark when responding to health emergencies, unable to direct resources where they're needed most or measure whether their efforts are actually working.

## Why Data Matters Now More Than Ever

WHO Director-General Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus put it simply: "Countries must be able to measure progress to make progress." During a pandemic, this principle becomes a matter of life and death. Accurate health data enables countries to understand their specific needs, track outbreaks, allocate limited resources efficiently, and coordinate response efforts across regions.

The SCORE package includes more than 90 universally-accepted tools and standards, all designed to work together seamlessly. It's not a one-size-fits-all solution but rather a flexible framework that countries can adapt to their own contexts and challenges.

The package gets its name from its core components: Survey, Count, Optimize, Review, and Enable. Together, these five elements create a complete system for improving how countries collect, analyze, and use health information. For the first time, governments have access to a single, coordinated resource that addresses all aspects of health information system performance.

## Building Better Systems From Crisis

COVID-19 has been a wake-up call about our collective vulnerability to health crises. But it's also an opportunity to rebuild stronger, more resilient systems. The SCORE Technical Package reflects this philosophy of building back better through collaboration and knowledge-sharing.

Dr Kelly Henning, Director of Public Health Programs at Bloomberg Philanthropies, emphasized that "using data to inform public health priorities and strategies has never been more important." Bloomberg Philanthropies has partnered with WHO to deliver this resource, recognizing that strengthening data systems is an investment in global [health security](https://infeeds.com/tags/health).

The initial release includes two foundational documents, with additional components coming later this year. Future releases will include the SCORE Assessment Instrument and detailed global, regional, and country status reports. Together, these materials will give countries everything they need to plan strategically, monitor progress against their own priorities, and track performance against global health targets like the Sustainable Development Goals.

## What This Means for Real Communities

On paper, stronger data systems sound abstract. In practice, they transform lives. When governments can accurately track disease patterns, they prevent outbreaks from becoming epidemics. When they can measure maternal mortality or vaccination rates, they know where to build clinics and train health workers. When they disaggregate data by region, gender, and age, they ensure that vulnerable populations don't get left behind.

Dr Samira Asma, WHO's Assistant Director-General for Data, Analytics and Delivery for Impact, stressed the urgency of action. "We must act now to invest in statistical systems in countries, respond to this pandemic and meet the SDGs and the Triple Billion targets," she said. "Our window of opportunity is closing."

The collaborative spirit behind SCORE matters as much as the tools themselves. Pandemic response isn't a competition between nations; it's a shared challenge requiring shared solutions. The package provides a common foundation of standards and practices that enables countries to learn from each other and work together effectively.

As global health faces mounting pressures from disease, climate change, and inequality, the capacity to collect, analyze, and act on data has become non-negotiable. If countries can successfully implement SCORE and use these tools to strengthen their health systems, the benefits will extend far beyond COVID-19: the question is whether we'll finally stop waiting for the next crisis to prove what we should have known all along.
