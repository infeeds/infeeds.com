Apple is taking a harder line on app store abuse. The company quietly tightened its developer guidelines over the weekend, introducing strict new rules around AI-generated content, user moderation, and what it calls bait-and-switch tactics that hide harmful features until after approval.

The move comes as pressure mounts on tech platforms to police their own ecosystems. San Francisco's city attorney David Chiu had already called out both Apple and Google in July for hosting nudification apps and profiting from app store fees. Now Apple is responding with updated legal language that leaves little room for interpretation.

## Blocking Hidden Features and Offensive Content

The updated guidelines explicitly prohibit apps that misrepresent their purpose during review only to reveal harmful capabilities after approval. This is the classic bait-and-switch: submit a photo editor, get approved, then quietly add nudification features. Apple says it will remove any app that contains features not present in the version submitted for review.

The company also strengthened language around user-generated content, requiring developers to filter objectionable material, provide reporting tools, and allow users to block abusive accounts. Apps hosting UGC now face mandatory moderation standards or removal.

More broadly, Apple updated its rules to ban apps using AI to create offensive content. While the exact scope remains somewhat open to interpretation, it's clearly aimed at preventing AI-powered harassment, deepfakes, and other synthetic abuse.

## The Scale of the Problem

Apple's enforcement stats reveal just how serious this has become. Last year, the company's review process blocked over 150,000 apps from reaching users entirely. That same year, Apple removed 193,000 developer accounts on account of fraud and abuse. These aren't edge cases; they're systemic challenges that require constant vigilance.

The nudification app problem, in particular, seemed to spiral out of control before regulators intervened. A Tech Transparency Project report in April showed both Apple and Google's app stores were hosting multiple nudify apps, some of which were even rated as suitable for minors. That's not just a moderation failure. That's a values failure.

## What This Means for Developers

For legitimate developers, these changes shouldn't matter much. If you're building tools with honest intent, the new guidelines just codify what should have been obvious: don't hide features, don't use AI to harass people, and moderate the content users create on your platform.

But for bad actors? The message is clear. Apple isn't waiting for city attorneys or advocacy groups to catch you anymore. The company is automating enforcement and making the rules explicit. Submit an app with hidden capabilities, and it gets removed. Period.

## The Bigger Picture

This isn't just about [tech](https://infeeds.com/tags/tech) giants policing themselves. It's about the role app stores play in society. These platforms have become essential infrastructure for how billions of people access software. When they host tools designed to harass, exploit, or deceive, they're making an implicit choice to enable harm.

Apple's updated guidelines suggest the company is finally taking that choice seriously. Whether the enforcement keeps pace with the policy remains to be seen. But at least the guardrails are now in place.

The question isn't whether Apple will catch every violation. They won't. The question is whether clearer rules and stronger enforcement create enough friction to deter the worst actors from even trying. In a space where [moderation](https://infeeds.com/tags/moderation) tools are constantly evolving and bad actors are constantly innovating, that's probably the best we can realistically hope for.
