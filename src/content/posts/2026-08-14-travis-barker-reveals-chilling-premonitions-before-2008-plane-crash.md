Travis Barker still can't believe he survived. The Blink-182 drummer recently shared haunting details about the moments leading up to his 2008 plane crash, revealing that both he and his young daughter sensed something was terribly wrong before he ever stepped foot on the aircraft.

During an appearance on Jay Shetty's "On Purpose" podcast, Barker recounted the eerie premonitions that preceded the tragedy. The day before the crash, his toddler daughter Alabama had a gut-wrenching reaction when saying goodbye.

"She's like, 'Dad, don't go!' She's bawling, and she doesn't usually do that," Barker explained. "She's like, 'The roof's gonna come off,' and I'm like, 'What is she talking about?'"

The cryptic warning from his daughter wasn't the only red flag. Barker had originally wanted to cancel the gig entirely, but couldn't back out in time. When he finally laid eyes on the small aircraft, an overwhelming sense of dread washed over him.

## A Feeling of Impending Doom

"Got on the plane, but I knew something feels off," the musician said. He was so convinced something terrible would happen that he actually called his father before takeoff with a devastating request: "If something happens to me, please make sure the kids are taken care of."

Those weren't casual words. Barker felt the weight of what was coming, even if he couldn't articulate exactly what it was. "I was saying way too much. All the signs were there," he reflected during the podcast appearance.

Seconds after the plane took off, everything spiraled into chaos. A deafening noise that sounded like gunshots filled the cabin, and the aircraft immediately lost control. Smoke began pouring into the passenger area as the plane jerked violently up and down before slamming into the runway.

"There was a full fire in the cabin," Barker said. "By then, I was just waiting for lights out. I was like, OK, this is really happening. I just couldn't believe it."

The crash claimed the lives of four people: both pilots, Barker's assistant, and his bodyguard. Barker and his good friend DJ AM (Adam Goldstein) were the only survivors among the passengers.

## The Aftermath and Living in Fear

While Barker survived the initial crash, the psychological scars cut deep. Just a year later, DJ AM died, and something shifted in Barker's mind. Survivor's guilt mixed with genuine terror consumed him. He became convinced he was next.

"I would go from my tour bus to the hotel, and no one would see me," Barker told People magazine. "I would just hide out. I was so afraid. I was just waiting for impact in the bus. I was just like, 'Oh, I'm next. I'm going to go anytime.'"

This wasn't rational paranoia. This was the very real aftermath of trauma, the kind that doesn't fade with time or therapy alone. For someone whose biggest fear was flying, surviving a plane crash only amplified that terror exponentially.

Barker's story serves as a powerful reminder of how our intuition sometimes picks up on dangers we can't consciously process. Whether you believe in premonitions or not, his experience speaks to the very real phenomenon of gut feelings before life-altering events. His daughter's inexplicable fear, his own sense of foreboding, the pilots' decision to fly that day - all of these elements converged into one of the most harrowing moments in [entertainment](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment) history.

Years later, Barker has spoken openly about his recovery, his faith, and how he's learned to live with the memories of that day. He's become an advocate for discussing mental health in the [celebrity](https://infeeds.com/tags/celebrity) world, proving that survival itself is only the beginning of the real battle.

What makes his account so compelling isn't just the near-death experience itself, but the fact that every part of him was screaming not to get on that plane, and he listened to none of it.
