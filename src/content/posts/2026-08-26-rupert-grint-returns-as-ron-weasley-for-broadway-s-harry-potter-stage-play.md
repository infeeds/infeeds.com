After 15 years away from the wizarding world, Rupert Grint is making a triumphant return to one of the most iconic roles in entertainment history. The actor will reprise his beloved character Ron Weasley in "Harry Potter and the Cursed Child" at Broadway's Lyric Theatre from February 2 through May 30, 2027. This time around, he'll be playing an adult version of Ron, bringing new depth to a character that has defined so much of his career.

The announcement comes hot on the heels of Tom Felton's successful run as an older Draco Malfoy, which proved audiences still have an insatiable appetite for the [entertainment](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment) phenomenon that shaped a generation. Felton's extended engagement through January 3, 2028, reportedly resulted in a significant box office boost, signaling strong demand for these nostalgic yet forward-looking casting choices.

## A Full-Circle Moment

For Grint, stepping back into Ron's shoes carries profound personal meaning. In a statement, he expressed the surreal nature of revisiting a character he's carried with him since childhood. "Ron Weasley has been a part of me since I was 11 years old and I can't wait to meet him again in this stage of life," Grint shared. "There is something so full-circle and so special about stepping back into Ron's shoes now when we are both fathers."

The actor emphasized how the experience feels simultaneously familiar and entirely new. Having spent decades growing up alongside the character through eight films, Grint brings not just acting talent but lived experience to this new iteration of Ron. The passage of time between his last appearance as the character and this theatrical return adds genuine gravity to an already emotionally resonant role.

## Bridging Past and Present

Producers Sonia Friedman and Colin Callender highlighted what makes Grint's return particularly special. They noted that he's not simply dusting off an old character from his past. Instead, he's continuing a journey that began when he was a child actor, carrying forward all the life experience and professional growth he's accumulated.

"His return creates a powerful bridge between the story audiences first fell in love with and the story of family, legacy and the passage of time that 'Harry Potter and the Cursed Child' carries forward," the producers explained. This perspective reframes the entire venture beyond nostalgia, positioning it as an organic evolution of storytelling that honors both the source material and the actors' real-world journeys.

Grint's career trajectory since the films concluded has been nothing short of impressive. He's worked across film, television, and acclaimed stage productions on both Broadway and the West End, demonstrating serious range beyond his iconic role. That credibility makes his return feel earned rather than desperate.

## Getting Tickets

Fans eager to see Grint's performance will have their chance soon. Presales for his 17-week run begin on September 1, with registration already available on the play's official website. The 17-week commitment represents a substantial engagement, giving audiences a genuine window to experience this landmark casting.

"Harry Potter and the Cursed Child" has proven itself as more than just a cash grab on an established brand. The play has earned critical acclaim and maintained strong audience interest since its debut, proving that the [entertainment](https://infeeds.com/tags/entertainment) world was hungry for more sophisticated storytelling within the wizarding universe.

The casting of Grint, following Felton's successful run, suggests we might see more beloved cast members returning to the stage. Whether this signals a deliberate strategy or serendipitous casting opportunities remains to be seen. Either way, it raises a fascinating question: what happens when beloved characters age alongside the actors who made them famous?
