<p>You booked every second Thursday. Then the kids got sick, then you were in Ottawa for a week, then a Thursday landed on a holiday, and somehow you haven't had a visit since the middle of June.</p>

<p>This is the actual failure mode of recurring cleaning. Not bad work. Drift.</p>

<p><strong>Skipping one visit costs more than you'd think</strong></p>

<p>Two weeks between cleans is maintenance. Four is catch up. Six and the crew is doing a small deep clean on a regular clean budget, so something gets cut, usually the thing you'd have picked last. That's why many <a href="https://puremaids.ca/" style="text-decoration:none;">cleaning services Toronto</a> homeowners rely on recommend adjusting the schedule instead of skipping appointments altogether. </p>

<p>The bathroom is where you notice first. Soap scum and hard water marks are easy at two weeks, stubborn at four, and genuinely hard past six. Same story with kitchen grease, which goes from a wipe to a scrub in about three weeks.</p>

<p>So if you have to skip, tell them to shorten the next gap rather than just resuming the rhythm. Most companies will slot you in.</p>

<p><strong>The rules worth knowing before you need them</strong></p>

<p>Ask about all of this at booking, when it's a boring administrative conversation instead of a stressful one:</p>

<p>Cancellation window. Twenty four hours is standard, forty eight in busier seasons. Inside that window most companies charge something, and that's fair, because the crew's day is already committed.</p>

<p>Vacation pauses. Some services suspend for a month and hold your slot. Some release it. Big difference if you're away for February.</p>

<p>Holidays. Ask which ones they close for and whether your slot moves forward or back.</p>

<p>Lockouts. If nobody's home and the fob fails, what then? Usually a fee, and fairly so, since a crew drove across the city.</p>

<p>Being away is the best time, not the worst</p>

<p>A lot of households cancel when they travel, which is backwards. An empty home is the easiest place to clean well. No dodging anyone working from the dining table, no waiting on a bathroom, no dog supervision.</p>

<p>Book the visit for the day before you fly back. Walking into a clean house after a flight is the best version of this whole arrangement, and it costs the same as any other Thursday.</p>

<p><strong>Three habits that stop the drift</strong></p>

<p>A <a href="https://puremaids.ca/service/regular" style="text-decoration:none;">recurring cleaning service</a> only works if the schedule survives contact with a normal month, so protect the schedule itself.</p>

<p>Put the visits in your calendar as real events, not a mental note. Pick a day that isn't already busy. Fridays before a long weekend get cancelled constantly. Decide up front what happens if you're not home, so nobody has to improvise.</p>

<p>Most drift is scheduling drift, and scheduling problems have scheduling solutions. Nothing to do with how well anyone cleans.</p>
